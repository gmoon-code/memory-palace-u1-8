# Unit 2 Review Flags

## RF-U2-001 — High

**Source:** PPT-S005

**Source claim:** “Organelles: membrane bound structures in eukaryotes.”

**Why flagged:** This treats membrane-boundedness as the universal definition of organelle, while the PPT itself later notes that ribosomes are sometimes classified differently.

**Resolution:** Use “membrane-bound organelles” for the relevant compartments; call ribosomes non-membrane subcellular structures as in the current CED.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-002 — High

**Source:** PPT-S007

**Source claim:** “Unique Cell Components”: plants—chloroplasts, central vacuole, cell wall, plasmodesmata; animals—lysosomes, centrosomes, flagella.

**Why flagged:** Several entries are not uniquely restricted to the listed kingdom; flagella occur in many eukaryotic lineages, centrosomes are not universal to animal cells, and plant lytic vacuoles perform lysosome-like functions.

**Resolution:** Teach these as common/characteristic examples in the course diagrams, not taxonomically unique universal traits.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-003 — High

**Source:** PPT-S009 / PPT-S011 / PPT-S013

**Source claim:** Nuclear pores regulate entry/exit “from the cell”; DNA must leave the nucleus by first being transcribed; free-ribosome proteins function only in cytosol.

**Why flagged:** The boundary is the nucleus, DNA generally remains nuclear, and free-ribosome products can be targeted to multiple non-endomembrane destinations.

**Resolution:** Canonical statements corrected using Campbell Ch.6; preserve source wording only in raw slide ledger.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-004 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S019

**Source claim:** “Peroxisomes: similar to lysosomes.”

**Why flagged:** Peroxisomes and lysosomes are distinct organelles with different enzyme systems and primary functions.

**Resolution:** Teach peroxisomes as oxidative compartments; distinguish from lysosomal hydrolysis.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-005 — High

**Source:** PPT-S025

**Source claim:** Mitochondria/chloroplasts are “capable of functioning on their own.”

**Why flagged:** Modern mitochondria and chloroplasts are highly host-dependent and are not free-living cells.

**Resolution:** Use own DNA/ribosomes/division and bacterial similarities as evidence; explicitly reject modern independent-living claim.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-006 — High

**Source:** PPT-S026

**Source claim:** Cristae “divide the mitochondria into two internal compartments.”

**Why flagged:** The inner mitochondrial membrane separates matrix from intermembrane space; cristae are folds of that membrane that increase area.

**Resolution:** Canonical cristae statement corrected.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-007 — High

**Source:** PPT-S027

**Source claim:** Matrix contains enzymes that “catalyze cellular respiration and produce ATP.”

**Why flagged:** The matrix contains citric-acid-cycle and other enzymes, but most oxidative-phosphorylation ATP synthesis occurs via ATP synthase in the inner membrane.

**Resolution:** Separate matrix reactions from inner-membrane electron transport/ATP synthesis.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-008 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S029

**Source claim:** “Light dependent reactions occur in grana.”

**Why flagged:** Light reactions occur in thylakoid membranes; grana are stacks of thylakoids.

**Resolution:** Use thylakoid membrane as the reaction location; grana remains structural vocabulary.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-009 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S034

**Source claim:** Microtubules grow from the centrosome.

**Why flagged:** Centrosomes are major microtubule-organizing centers in many animal cells, but not a universal eukaryotic rule.

**Resolution:** Qualify to animal cells/microtubule-organizing centers.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-010 — Low

**Source:** PPT-S037

**Source claim:** Intermediate filaments are “permanent structural elements.”

**Why flagged:** Intermediate filaments are comparatively stable but still dynamic and remodelable.

**Resolution:** Use “relatively stable structural elements.”

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-011 — High

**Source:** PPT-S055

**Source claim:** Integral proteins are “aka transmembrane proteins.”

**Why flagged:** Transmembrane proteins are a subset of integral proteins; some integral proteins do not span the entire bilayer.

**Resolution:** Distinguish integral from transmembrane.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-012 — High

**Source:** PPT-S058

**Source claim:** Plant cell wall provides “regulation of water intake.”

**Why flagged:** The wall mechanically resists expansion and contributes to water relations, while selective membrane transport is governed primarily by the plasma membrane.

**Resolution:** Canonical cell-wall function corrected.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-013 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S068 / PPT-S102

**Source claim:** Osmosis described as water moving from low solute to high solute, and water-potential slide lists separate low-solute/high-solute and high-pressure/low-pressure rules.

**Why flagged:** These are useful under restricted conditions, but net water movement is governed by total water potential when both solute and pressure differ.

**Resolution:** Use Ψ high→low as the governing rule; low-solute→high-solute only when other water-potential components are equal.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-014 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S078

**Source claim:** Unequal ion concentrations “result in an electrical charge.”

**Why flagged:** Membrane potential is a voltage from charge separation across a membrane; bulk solutions remain nearly electrically neutral.

**Resolution:** Canonical membrane-potential statement corrected.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-015 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S077

**Source claim:** ATP transfers a terminal phosphate to “the transport protein” as the general active-transport mechanism.

**Why flagged:** Direct phosphorylation occurs in some pumps, including P-type ATPases, but is not universal to all active transport.

**Resolution:** Use this mechanism for the Na+/K+ ATPase example, not as the definition of all active transport.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-016 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S089 / PPT-S091

**Source claim:** Endocytosis described as uptake “from vesicles fused from the plasma membrane”; pinocytosis defined as uptake in a protein-coated vesicle.

**Why flagged:** Endocytic vesicles form by membrane invagination/pinching off; pinocytosis is not defined by a single coating mechanism.

**Resolution:** Canonical bulk-transport statements corrected.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-017 — Medium

**Source:** PPT-S100

**Source claim:** Hypertonic conditions cause cells to “shrivel and die.”

**Why flagged:** Cells lose water and shrink; death depends on severity, duration, and cell type.

**Resolution:** Teach shrinkage/plasmolysis as direct effects; do not make death inevitable.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-018 — High

**Source:** APBIO-U2-Packet pp.1–5

**Source claim:** Packet uses legacy AP codes SYI-1.D through EVO-1.B and eleven topic numbers.

**Why flagged:** The current Fall 2025 CED uses Unit 2 Topics 2.1–2.10 with revised learning-objective/essential-knowledge codes and some scope redistribution.

**Resolution:** Preserve legacy packet codes as historical teacher-source metadata; map all canonical records to current 2025 CED topics/codes.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-019 — Scope

**Source:** PPT-S031–037; packet vocabulary

**Source claim:** Cytoskeleton, centrosome, actin, intermediate filaments, detailed chloroplast/mitochondrial subcompartments and several transport subtypes receive extensive teacher coverage.

**Why flagged:** Much of this detail is not explicitly required in the current 2025 CED Unit 2 essential knowledge.

**Resolution:** Keep as TEACHER_REQUIRED_ENRICHMENT where classroom responsibility is clear; do not label it AP-required solely because it appears in teacher materials.

**Status:** RESOLVED

## RF-U2-020 — Scope

**Source:** PPT-S042 statement

**Source claim:** “Remember: all formulas are given to you on the AP exam.”

**Why flagged:** The current CED lists relevant equations, but a blanket promise that every formula will always be supplied should not become a scientific/curricular memory target.

**Resolution:** Retain formulas and calculation fluency; omit the blanket guarantee from canonical student science.

**Status:** RESOLVED
