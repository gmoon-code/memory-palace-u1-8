# Unit 5 F1 Review Flags

All source claims remain preserved in the raw slide ledger. These flags record where the canonical layer corrects, qualifies, or limits source wording before mnemonic generation.

## RF-U5-001 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S002
**Source claim / issue**: Genes are described simply as DNA segments that “code for basic units of heredity.”
**Resolution**: Use gene as a DNA sequence at a locus whose expression or regulatory function contributes to a functional product/trait; not all genes encode proteins.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-002 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S004
**Source claim / issue**: Asexual offspring are called exact copies and mutation is said to be the only source of variation.
**Resolution**: Asexual reproduction often yields genetically very similar clonal descendants, but mutation, mitotic recombination, genome rearrangements, and environmental effects can create genetic or phenotypic variation. Do not encode “exact” or mutation-only as universal.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-003 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S004
**Source claim / issue**: Sexual reproduction is defined as requiring two parents.
**Resolution**: Sexual reproduction involves fusion of gametes and can occur by self-fertilization in some organisms; two separate parents are common but not universal.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-004 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S005
**Source claim / issue**: Homologous chromosomes are said to carry the “same genetic information.”
**Resolution**: Homologous chromosomes carry corresponding genes/loci in similar order but can contain different alleles; distinguish homologs from sister chromatids.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-005 — LOW — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S007
**Source claim / issue**: A karyotype is described only as chromosome pairs ordered by size and length.
**Resolution**: A karyotype is an organized display of chromosomes typically paired and arranged using size, centromere position, and banding/morphology; the exact arrangement is an analytical convention.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-006 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S009
**Source claim / issue**: Chromosomes are divided universally into autosomes and X/Y “sex chromosomes.”
**Resolution**: Treat autosome versus X/Y as a human/XX-XY model. Chromosomal sex-determination systems vary, and chromosome complement should not be equated with gender or a complete account of sex development.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-007 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S009
**Source claim / issue**: All sexually reproducing organisms are said to have both a diploid and haploid number.
**Resolution**: Sexual life cycles vary. AP requires chromosome transmission and excludes memorization of detailed plant/animal life cycles; do not encode a universal diploid-dominant life cycle.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-008 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S010
**Source claim / issue**: Fertilization is defined only as sperm fusing with egg.
**Resolution**: Use fusion of haploid gametes as the general process; sperm and egg are appropriate examples for anisogamous organisms, including humans.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-009 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S013
**Source claim / issue**: Mitosis is stated to occur in somatic cells and always produce two diploid genetically identical daughter cells.
**Resolution**: Mitosis segregates replicated chromosomes and conserves chromosome-set number; it can occur in haploid or diploid cells. Genetic identity is expected barring mutation/segregation error, and cytokinesis is a separate process.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-010 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S016, PPT-S020
**Source claim / issue**: Crossing over is described as DNA exchange “between homologous pairs,” and every resulting chromatid is said to be unique.
**Resolution**: Crossing over exchanges corresponding DNA between nonsister chromatids of homologous chromosomes. Sister chromatids involved in crossovers can become nonidentical, but do not encode “every chromatid is unique” as an absolute.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-011 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S018, PPT-S023
**Source claim / issue**: The haploid state after meiosis I can be misread as meaning chromosomes are unreplicated.
**Resolution**: After meiosis I, each cell is haploid with one homolog from each pair, but each chromosome still consists of two sister chromatids; DNA content and chromosome-set number are distinct quantities.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-012 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S019
**Source claim / issue**: “No crossing over” is listed under prophase II without emphasizing the more important interdivision rule.
**Resolution**: Crossing over normally occurs during prophase I. No DNA replication occurs between meiosis I and meiosis II; emphasize this relation rather than memorizing a prohibition label.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-013 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S022
**Source claim / issue**: Meiosis is said to yield four haploid daughter cells that are each genetically unique as a universal gamete outcome.
**Resolution**: Meiosis typically produces four genetically distinct haploid nuclei/cells in the standard model; gametogenesis can modify the cellular products, as in unequal oogenesis. Keep AP focus on haploid chromosome products.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-014 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S024, PPT-S025
**Source claim / issue**: Random fertilization is grouped as if it is a mechanism of meiosis and meiosis/fertilization are said to “ensure” diversity.
**Resolution**: Crossing over and independent assortment occur during meiosis; fertilization is subsequent. Together sexual reproduction processes increase genetic variation rather than guarantee a particular amount of diversity.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-015 — LOW — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S027
**Source claim / issue**: The genetic code is said to be shared by all living systems.
**Resolution**: The genetic code is nearly universal, with known variants such as some organelle genetic codes. Use conserved/nearly universal language as evidence of common ancestry.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-016 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S031
**Source claim / issue**: Capital and lowercase letters are said to denote dominant and recessive “traits.”
**Resolution**: Letter case is a notation convention for alleles in simple genetics problems. Dominance is a relationship between alleles for a phenotype, not a property of a trait label itself.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-017 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S032
**Source claim / issue**: Phenotype is said to be determined by genotype alone.
**Resolution**: Phenotype results from genotype expressed in a developmental/environmental context; Unit 5.5 explicitly requires environmental effects and phenotypic plasticity.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-018 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S033
**Source claim / issue**: The text says testcrosses identify homozygous dominant versus heterozygous without stating the tester genotype.
**Resolution**: A classic testcross mates an individual with a dominant phenotype and unknown genotype to a homozygous recessive individual; offspring phenotypes provide evidence about the unknown genotype.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-019 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S036, PPT-S037, PPT-S038
**Source claim / issue**: Dominant and recessive are repeatedly phrased as properties of whole traits and classic F2 results can read as universal.
**Resolution**: Apply dominant/recessive to allele relationships in a defined phenotype. The classic 3:1 F2 ratio requires a single gene, two alleles, complete dominance, segregation, and the modeled cross.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-020 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S038
**Source claim / issue**: Mendel’s model says a recessive allele has “no noticeable effect” whenever alleles differ.
**Resolution**: That wording applies to complete dominance for the scored phenotype. It is not a general property of recessive alleles across molecular or quantitative phenotypes.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-021 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S042
**Source claim / issue**: Independent assortment is stated as genes for one trait simply not being inherited with genes of another trait.
**Resolution**: Independent assortment describes independent segregation of allele pairs for unlinked genes, especially genes on different chromosomes; linked genes can deviate, and far-apart loci on one chromosome can behave approximately unlinked.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-022 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S043, PPT-S052
**Source claim / issue**: A 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio is presented as the outcome of a dihybrid cross without conditions.
**Resolution**: The 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio is expected for AaBb × AaBb only under complete dominance at both loci, independent assortment, and standard probability assumptions.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-023 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S060
**Source claim / issue**: The pedigree slide says a dominant trait cannot skip a generation and one parent must show it, and says males are more commonly affected for X-linked traits generally.
**Resolution**: These are heuristic patterns with exceptions. De novo variants, penetrance, phenotype misclassification, and family size can alter pedigrees. Male-biased occurrence is especially associated with X-linked recessive models, not all X-linked traits.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-024 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S064
**Source claim / issue**: Incomplete dominance is described as the F1 phenotype being a “mix” of parental phenotypes.
**Resolution**: Use intermediate heterozygous phenotype; alleles remain discrete and do not blend genetically.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-025 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S074, PPT-S075, PPT-S076, PPT-S078
**Source claim / issue**: Sex-linked material uses male/female as direct synonyms for XY/XX chromosome complement.
**Resolution**: For inheritance calculations, state the chromosome-complement model explicitly (for example XX/XY). Do not infer gender or the full biology of sex development from chromosome complement; acknowledge other sex-determination systems where relevant.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-026 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S076
**Source claim / issue**: An X-linked recessive allele is said to require homozygosity in all females and one copy in all males.
**Resolution**: In the standard XX/XY pedigree model, an XX individual generally requires two recessive alleles for the classic affected phenotype and an XY individual can express an X-linked recessive phenotype with one allele. X-inactivation, chromosome variation, penetrance, and other biology can complicate real phenotypes.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-027 — MEDIUM — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S078
**Source claim / issue**: X inactivation is explained as females having “double” the X chromosomes of males.
**Resolution**: In typical mammalian XX cells, one X chromosome is largely inactivated early in development as dosage compensation relative to cells with one X. Phrase this as an XX/XY model, not a universal statement about all females and males.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-028 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S085
**Source claim / issue**: One map unit is treated as exactly equal to 1% recombination over all distances.
**Resolution**: For relatively short intervals, 1% recombinant offspring corresponds to about 1 map unit (1 cM). Multiple crossovers cause recombination frequency to underestimate longer map distances, and observed recombination frequency approaches a maximum of 50%.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-029 — HIGH — SCIENCE
**Source slides**: PPT-S088
**Source claim / issue**: Animal mitochondrial DNA is said to be ALL maternally inherited and plant mitochondria/chloroplasts always transmitted by ovule, not pollen.
**Resolution**: Use current CED wording: animal mitochondrial inheritance is usually/typically maternal, and plant mitochondrial/chloroplast inheritance is often/predominantly maternal. Avoid absolutes.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-030 — HIGH — STATISTICS
**Source slides**: PPT-S092, PPT-S100
**Source claim / issue**: The chi-square null hypothesis and p-value are described as “data will match expected results” and p=0.05 as 95% confidence that observed data fit extremely well.
**Resolution**: The null states that deviations from expected counts are attributable to sampling/random variation under the model. A p-value is the probability, assuming the null/model, of obtaining a chi-square statistic at least as extreme as observed; 0.05 is a significance threshold, not 95% confidence that the model is true.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-031 — HIGH — STATISTICS
**Source slides**: PPT-S102
**Source claim / issue**: The decision rule says ACCEPT the null hypothesis when chi-square is below the critical value.
**Resolution**: Use “fail to reject the null hypothesis.” A nonsignificant result does not prove or accept the null as true.
**Status**: RESOLVED

## RF-U5-032 — MEDIUM — STATISTICS

**Source claim / issue**: Degrees of freedom are presented universally as categories minus one.
**Resolution**: For the AP goodness-of-fit tasks shown, df = number of outcome categories − 1 when expected proportions are specified and no additional parameters are estimated. Keep the formula in that task context.
**Status**: RESOLVED
