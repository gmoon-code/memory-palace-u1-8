## Important source-to-canonical corrections

F1 preserves the teacher wording while preventing later mnemonic prose from encoding several oversimplifications. The most important include homologous chromosomes carrying the same loci but potentially different alleles; asexual descendants not being literally exact in every respect; sexual reproduction not universally requiring two separate parents; chromosome complement not being a synonym for gender or a complete description of sex development; mitosis not universally producing diploid cells; crossing over occurring between nonsister chromatids; independent assortment applying to unlinked genes; 3:1 and 9:3:3:1 ratios being conditional; X-linked pedigree heuristics being model-dependent; map units being approximations of recombination distance; organelle inheritance being usually/typically rather than absolutely maternal; and chi-square requiring reject/fail-to-reject language with correct p-value interpretation.
