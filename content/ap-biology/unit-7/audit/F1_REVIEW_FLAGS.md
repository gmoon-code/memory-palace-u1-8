# Unit 7 F1 Review Flags

All **39 / 39** flags are resolved. Raw teacher wording remains preserved in `source/canonical-unit7-f1.json`; the resolutions below govern canonical and future student-facing science.

## RF-U7-001 · HISTORY · MEDIUM
Slides: 2, 3, 4

**Source issue**  The opening historical sequence can make Darwin’s theory appear to derive mainly from Galápagos observations and uses historical language such as “good,” “bad,” and “improvement.”

**Locked resolution**  Preserve the quote and Galápagos material as historical context. Canonical science must avoid teleology and present natural selection as differential reproductive success acting on heritable variation, with Darwin’s evidence coming from many observations including biogeography, fossils, artificial selection, and species variation.

## RF-U7-002 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 8

**Source issue**  The deck says natural selection acts on random mutations, which narrows the target of selection and can imply that mutation alone supplies all relevant variation.

**Locked resolution**  Lock the distinction that mutation is random with respect to fitness and introduces new alleles, while natural selection acts nonrandomly on heritable phenotypic variation. Recombination and sexual reproduction also generate variation from existing alleles.

## RF-U7-003 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 9

**Source issue**  The sickle-cell example says sickle cell anemia protects against malaria, conflating sickle-cell disease with the heterozygous sickle-cell trait.

**Locked resolution**  Use the heterozygote-advantage framing: in malaria-endemic environments, individuals heterozygous for the sickle-cell allele can have higher fitness than either homozygote. Do not present sickle-cell disease itself as the protective state.

## RF-U7-004 · TERMINOLOGY · MEDIUM
Slides: 10, 11

**Source issue**  Descent with modification is presented as the modern definition of evolution.

**Locked resolution**  Retain descent with modification as Darwin’s historical framing. For population genetics, define evolution operationally as change in allele frequencies or heritable population characteristics across generations.

## RF-U7-005 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 15

**Source issue**  The deck emphasizes traits that lead to survival accumulating in the population.

**Locked resolution**  Use differential reproductive success as the core mechanism. Survival matters when it changes the number of viable offspring contributed to later generations.

## RF-U7-006 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 17

**Source issue**  The natural-versus-artificial selection slide says both can lead to evolutionary change in the organism.

**Locked resolution**  Canonical wording must state that populations evolve across generations; individual organisms do not evolve genetically during their lifetime.

## RF-U7-007 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 22

**Source issue**  The deck groups natural selection with 'random occurrences' that drive evolution.

**Locked resolution**  Separate stochastic mechanisms from selection. Mutation occurrence and genetic drift are random with respect to fitness; gene flow depends on movement; natural selection is nonrandom differential reproductive success among heritable phenotypes.

## RF-U7-008 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 23

**Source issue**  The deck states that mutation rates tend to be fast in prokaryotes because of faster generation time.

**Locked resolution**  Distinguish mutation rate per replication from the number of generations per unit time. Short generation times can make evolutionary change appear quickly without implying a universally higher mutation probability per nucleotide per replication.

## RF-U7-009 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 23

**Source issue**  The deck states that most mutations fall in the neutral-to-harmful range.

**Locked resolution**  Use a cautious formulation: many mutations are neutral or nearly neutral with respect to fitness, deleterious mutations occur, and beneficial mutations are comparatively rare; the distribution depends on genomic context and environment.

## RF-U7-010 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 24, 25, 26

**Source issue**  Bottleneck and founder effects are taught as named subtypes of drift but can be mistaken for adaptive processes.

**Locked resolution**  Lock that both are sampling effects that change allele frequencies by chance, can reduce variation, and do not consistently produce adaptation.

## RF-U7-011 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 36

**Source issue**  Sexual selection is described as males having 'useless structures' simply because females choose them.

**Locked resolution**  Define sexual selection as differential reproductive success arising from mate choice or competition for mates. Sexually selected traits can carry survival costs but are not biologically 'useless,' and mating patterns are not restricted to female choice of male ornaments.

## RF-U7-012 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 37, 38, 39, 40

**Source issue**  The Hardy-Weinberg sequence implies that violation of any condition necessarily means microevolution and that any genotype-frequency deviation proves allele-frequency evolution.

**Locked resolution**  Treat Hardy-Weinberg as a locus-specific null model. Nonrandom mating alone can change genotype frequencies without changing allele frequencies. A deviation prompts evaluation of which assumptions are violated and whether allele frequencies changed.

## RF-U7-013 · TERMINOLOGY · HIGH
Slides: 41, 42, 43, 44

**Source issue**  The deck labels p as the dominant allele and q as the recessive allele.

**Locked resolution**  Lock p and q as frequencies of two alleles, with no inherent dominance meaning. Dominance affects phenotype expression, not which symbol must represent an allele.

## RF-U7-014 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 43, 46, 47, 48

**Source issue**  The problem-solving heuristic treats phenotype or individual-frequency information as automatically equal to p², 2pq, or q².

**Locked resolution**  Only equate genotype frequencies with p², 2pq, and q² under Hardy-Weinberg assumptions. Inferring q² from a recessive phenotype also assumes the phenotype reliably identifies the recessive homozygous genotype.

## RF-U7-015 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 49

**Source issue**  The genotype-count example takes square roots of observed homozygote frequencies to estimate p and q.

**Locked resolution**  Calculate allele frequencies directly from genotype counts: p=(2N_AA+N_Aa)/(2N) and q=(2N_aa+N_Aa)/(2N). Square-root shortcuts apply only when Hardy-Weinberg genotype proportions are justified.

## RF-U7-016 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 51

**Source issue**  The diversity slide can be read as 'more diversity is always better.'

**Locked resolution**  Preserve the CED relationship that genetic diversity generally increases the probability that a population contains variants able to withstand a novel pressure, while avoiding an absolute claim that every increase in variation is beneficial.

## RF-U7-017 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 54

**Source issue**  Carbon-14 is presented as a general fossil-dating method.

**Locked resolution**  Lock radiometric dating to isotope-appropriate timescales. Carbon-14 is useful only for relatively recent organic remains; older fossils are commonly constrained using older surrounding rock layers and longer-lived radioisotopes.

## RF-U7-018 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 55, 60

**Source issue**  Vestigial structures are defined as structures that no longer have a use, with the human coccyx and appendix as examples.

**Locked resolution**  Define vestigial structures as inherited remnants that have lost much or all of an ancestral function and may retain reduced or modified functions. Do not teach the appendix or coccyx as functionless.

## RF-U7-019 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 57

**Source issue**  Bird, bat, and bee wings are grouped together as analogous structures without distinguishing the homologous forelimb skeleton of birds and bats.

**Locked resolution**  When discussing flight structures, distinguish analogous wing function from homologous tetrapod forelimb ancestry. Bird and bat forelimbs share homologous skeletal elements even though powered flight evolved independently.

## RF-U7-020 · TERMINOLOGY · MEDIUM
Slides: 63

**Source issue**  Phylogenetics is defined as a hypothesis of evolutionary history.

**Locked resolution**  Use phylogeny for the evolutionary history/relationship hypothesis and phylogenetics for the methods used to infer and analyze those relationships.

## RF-U7-021 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 65

**Source issue**  The deck says phylogenetic trees differ from cladograms because trees show change over time measured by fossils.

**Locked resolution**  Use the current CED distinction: some phylogenetic trees are scaled to time or amount of change and may be calibrated using fossils or molecular clocks; cladograms communicate branching order without a time/change scale.

## RF-U7-022 · TERMINOLOGY · LOW
Slides: 67

**Source issue**  The term basal taxon can be misread as meaning an extant lineage is primitive or ancestral to other living taxa.

**Locked resolution**  Preserve the classroom term as enrichment but avoid primitive/advanced ranking. A lineage that branches near the root is an extant lineage with an earlier divergence in the represented tree, not the ancestor of the other extant taxa.

## RF-U7-023 · SCOPE · MEDIUM
Slides: 70, 71

**Source issue**  Monophyletic/paraphyletic/polyphyletic group labels and the principle of parsimony are taught explicitly, but these exact terms are not required in the current Unit 7 CED.

**Locked resolution**  Retain as teacher-required enrichment. Current AP-required phylogeny focuses on nodes, shared derived characters, outgroups, molecular/morphological evidence, and revisable tree hypotheses.

## RF-U7-024 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 71

**Source issue**  Parsimony is framed as the rule to use whenever characters conflict.

**Locked resolution**  Teach parsimony as one inference principle that favors fewer evolutionary changes. Modern phylogenetics also uses statistical methods; do not imply parsimony always yields the correct tree.

## RF-U7-025 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 77

**Source issue**  The biological species definition is presented without its domain limitation.

**Locked resolution**  Apply the biological species concept to sexually reproducing organisms and note that it is difficult or inapplicable for asexual organisms and many fossil taxa.

## RF-U7-026 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 79, 80

**Source issue**  Sympatric speciation is described as usually resulting from exploitation of a new niche, while allopatric speciation is tied mainly to physical disasters.

**Locked resolution**  Lock geography as the defining contrast. Allopatric isolation can arise through many geographic processes; sympatric speciation can arise through mechanisms such as polyploidy, habitat differentiation, or sexual selection.

## RF-U7-027 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 93

**Source issue**  Reduced hybrid fertility is said to usually result from differences in parental chromosome number.

**Locked resolution**  Chromosome-number or structural differences can cause hybrid sterility, but genic incompatibilities and meiotic problems can also reduce fertility. Keep the mule as an example without making chromosome number the universal cause.

## RF-U7-028 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 94

**Source issue**  Hybrid breakdown is defined as later-generation offspring becoming sterile.

**Locked resolution**  Use the broader definition: first-generation hybrids may be viable and fertile, while later-generation hybrids can show reduced viability, reduced fertility, or both.

## RF-U7-029 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 99

**Source issue**  Divergent evolution is written as if accumulating differences necessarily results in a new species.

**Locked resolution**  Divergence can occur within or among populations and may contribute to speciation when reproductive isolation evolves; divergence does not automatically create a new species.

## RF-U7-030 · SCOPE · MEDIUM
Slides: 97, 100

**Source issue**  Macroevolution, five mass extinctions, and human-driven extinction-rate detail are included beyond the explicit current Unit 7 required statements.

**Locked resolution**  Preserve as teacher enrichment and conceptual context. Do not make memorization of the number of mass extinctions a Unit 7 AP requirement.

## RF-U7-031 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 102

**Source issue**  The slide labels the earliest 3.5-billion-year fossil evidence as cyanobacteria.

**Locked resolution**  Use the current CED date for earliest fossil evidence without requiring a definitive cyanobacterial identity. Ancient microbial fossils and stromatolites support early life, but assigning all earliest evidence specifically to cyanobacteria is stronger than the CED requires.

## RF-U7-032 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 103

**Source issue**  The deck says abundant oxygen helped inorganic molecules synthesize organic molecules on early Earth.

**Locked resolution**  Correct this directly. Early Earth had little free molecular oxygen. Plausible energy sources for abiotic organic synthesis include lightning, ultraviolet radiation, volcanism, and geochemical gradients; oxygen was not abundant.

## RF-U7-033 · HISTORY · HIGH
Slides: 104

**Source issue**  The Oparin-Haldane atmosphere is presented as if its hydrogen/methane/ammonia/water composition is the established composition of early Earth.

**Locked resolution**  Present the strongly reducing atmosphere as the historical hypothesis tested by Miller. Current evidence supports an early atmosphere with substantial nitrogen and carbon dioxide and little oxygen, while locally reducing volcanic conditions may also have occurred.

## RF-U7-034 · SCIENCE · HIGH
Slides: 104, 105

**Source issue**  The Miller-Urey sequence can imply the experiment produced macromolecules or demonstrated the origin of life.

**Locked resolution**  Lock the result to abiotic synthesis of small organic molecules, including amino acids, under simulated conditions. Macromolecule formation, protocells, and self-replicating systems are separate origin-of-life steps.

## RF-U7-035 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 106

**Source issue**  The RNA-world slide is too brief to cover the current CED requirements.

**Locked resolution**  Add the three AP-required assumptions: RNA replication must permit genetic continuity, complementary base pairing is required for templated replication, and genetically encoded proteins were not initially required as catalysts.

## RF-U7-036 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 107

**Source issue**  The endosymbiont-theory review says mitochondria and chloroplasts may have been engulfed by eukaryotic cells and can blur organelle origins with the origin of the first life.

**Locked resolution**  Reuse the Unit 2 endosymbiosis lock: mitochondria and chloroplasts descend from formerly free-living prokaryotes incorporated into ancestral host cells. Endosymbiosis explains later eukaryotic evolution, not the initial origin of life.

## RF-U7-037 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 25

**Source issue**  The bottleneck effect is defined as a drastic reduction caused by a non-selective disaster, which is narrower than the current-CED mechanism and is inconsistent with the slide's inclusion of hunting as an example.

**Locked resolution**  Define a bottleneck as genetic drift following a severe reduction in population size when survivors are a chance, nonrepresentative sample with respect to the focal alleles. A demographic decline can have selective and nonselective components; only the sampling component is genetic drift.

## RF-U7-038 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 72

**Source issue**  The tree-reading answer labels 'butterflies, moths, and flies' collectively as sister taxa, which can blur the requirement that sister taxa or sister clades are two lineages that share an immediate common ancestor.

**Locked resolution**  Lock sister taxa/sister clades as the two descendant lineages emerging from the same immediate node. In the displayed tree, the butterflies-and-moths lineage/clade is sister to the fly lineage at node C.

## RF-U7-039 · SCIENCE · MEDIUM
Slides: 30

**Source issue**  The Amish founder-effect example says a small number of founders carried an 'unusual number of mutations,' which incorrectly emphasizes mutation count rather than chance sampling of rare alleles.

**Locked resolution**  Describe the founder effect as a small founding group carrying a nonrepresentative set of alleles, so some rare alleles can become unusually common in the descendant population. Do not imply the founders necessarily had more mutations overall.
