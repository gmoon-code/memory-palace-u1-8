## High-priority scientific repairs locked in F1

- Separates **random mutation and genetic drift** from **nonrandom natural selection**.
- Corrects the Amish founder-effect example from an “unusual number of mutations” to **chance sampling of rare alleles** in a small founding population.
- Corrects the sickle-cell example to **heterozygote advantage in malaria-endemic environments**, without treating sickle-cell disease itself as the protective state.
- Keeps evolutionary fitness tied to **relative reproductive success**, not strength, health, speed, or survival alone.
- Protects the rule that **populations evolve across generations; individuals do not genetically evolve during their lifetimes**.
- Corrects the teacher source's grouping of natural selection with “random occurrences.”
- Distinguishes short generation time from a universally high **per-replication mutation rate**.
- Locks genetic drift, bottleneck effect, and founder effect as **nonadaptive sampling processes**.
- Qualifies a bottleneck as drift only to the extent that survivors are a **chance, nonrepresentative sample**; a population decline can also contain selective components.
- Rewrites sexual selection around **mate choice and competition for mates** without treating sexually selected traits as biologically useless or restricting the mechanism to female choice of male ornaments.
- Repairs Hardy–Weinberg interpretation: **nonrandom mating alone can change genotype frequencies without changing allele frequencies**.
- Removes the shortcut that **p must be dominant and q recessive**.
- Corrects allele-frequency calculation from observed genotype counts to **direct allele counting**, rather than square-rooting observed homozygote frequencies.
- Qualifies carbon-14 as a radiometric tool for **relatively recent organic remains**, not all fossils.
- Defines vestigial structures as ancestral remnants that may retain **reduced or modified functions**.
- Distinguishes analogous flight from homologous tetrapod forelimb ancestry in the bird/bat/insect wing example.
- Qualifies phylogenetic terminology so early-branching lineages are not treated as primitive or ancestral to other living taxa.
- Corrects the tree-reading example so **sister taxa/sister clades are the two descendant lineages from the same immediate node**.
- Applies the biological species concept specifically to **sexually reproducing organisms** and preserves its limitations.
- Broadens sympatric speciation beyond niche exploitation and broadens reduced hybrid fertility beyond chromosome-count differences alone.
- Corrects the early-Earth claim that prebiotic synthesis occurred with **abundant oxygen**; early Earth had little free molecular oxygen.
- Treats the Oparin–Haldane atmosphere as a historical hypothesis and Miller–Urey as evidence for **abiotic formation of small organic molecules**, not production of life or macromolecules.
- Adds all three current-CED **RNA-world assumptions**: replication for genetic continuity, complementary base pairing, and catalysis before genetically encoded proteins.

## Current-CED coverage additions

The teacher deck covers the overall current Unit 7 sequence well, but F1 makes several CED relationships explicit so future stories cannot omit them:

- molecular variation within cells can connect to organismal fitness;
- gene flow tends to counter population divergence;
- Hardy–Weinberg is a **locus-specific null model**;
- molecular data are generally stronger than morphology alone for reconstructing phylogeny;
- the **outgroup** is explicitly locked as an AP-required reference lineage used to infer ancestral versus derived states;
- phylogenetic trees and cladograms are **revisable hypotheses**;
- environmental context can reverse the fitness effect of an allele;
- the RNA-world hypothesis requires the three explicit assumptions named in Topic 7.12.
