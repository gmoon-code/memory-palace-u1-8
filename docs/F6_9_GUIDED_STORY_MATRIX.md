## Story architecture by palace

### Z1 — The Evidence Lockdown

**Tagline.** A strange result has sealed the lab. Build a study strong enough to open every door.

**Premise.** A glowing plant has appeared behind the Observation Window, but the lab security system refuses to accept anyone’s explanation. Each room will open only when the investigation becomes more trustworthy.

**Mission.** Carry one evidence packet from the first observation to the final sampling balcony without letting bias, bad controls, or weak statistics corrupt it.

**Guide.** Dr. Vale, the night-shift research director

**Route.** Observation Window → Hypothesis Doors → Control Benches → Variable Control Panel → Bias Goggles Cabinet → Statistics Reporter Desk → Center-of-Data Roundabout → Variability Floor → Sampling Balcony.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Observation Window → Hypothesis Doors**. The plant’s sensor spits out a blank evidence card. Two locked doors light up ahead, demanding a testable prediction before the card can move on.
- **Hypothesis Doors → Control Benches**. When the hypotheses are framed as testable alternatives, the doors release the evidence card onto a row of waiting Control Benches.
- **Control Benches → Variable Control Panel**. Once the comparison groups are separated cleanly, power flows from the benches into the Variable Control Panel.
- **Variable Control Panel → Bias Goggles Cabinet**. The finished protocol prints onto a visor tray. Dr. Vale sends you straight to the Bias Goggles Cabinet before anyone is allowed to observe results.
- **Bias Goggles Cabinet → Statistics Reporter Desk**. With expectations masked where appropriate, the result sheet slides under a shutter and lands on the Statistics Reporter Desk.
- **Statistics Reporter Desk → Center-of-Data Roundabout**. The descriptive reporter rolls the raw data toward the Center-of-Data Roundabout, where three competing summaries are waiting.
- **Center-of-Data Roundabout → Variability Floor**. The three center markers settle, but the floor beneath them starts pulling apart. The data are forcing you to confront variability next.
- **Variability Floor → Sampling Balcony**. The spreading tiles open into a staircase. At the top, the Sampling Balcony shows why sample means carry their own uncertainty.

**Payoff.** The final error-bar display settles into place. Dr. Vale stamps the evidence packet APPROVED, the lockdown lights turn green, and the lab doors open. You leave with one continuous investigation in your head, from observation to uncertainty.

### Z2 — The Midnight Chemistry Museum

**Tagline.** The exhibits have come alive, and an unstable atom has knocked out the foyer lights.

**Premise.** At midnight, the Chemistry Foyer wakes up. Matter statues move, ions steal coats, and the bond exhibits begin pulling at one another. A power failure has scrambled the museum labels.

**Mission.** Restore the museum by following matter from atoms and periodic patterns into the bonds and elements that living systems use.

**Guide.** Mina, the museum’s slightly frantic curator

**Route.** Matter Statue → Element–Compound Counter → Isotope Scale → Periodic Elevator → Octopus Valence Chandelier → Electronegativity Magnet Wall → Covalent Tug-of-War Table → Ion Coat Check → Hydrogen-Bond Bridge → Macromolecule Element Crates → Element-Role Workbench.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Matter Statue → Element–Compound Counter**. A loose exhibit tag skitters across the floor and stops at the Element–Compound Counter, where the museum starts sorting what matter is made from.
- **Element–Compound Counter → Isotope Scale**. A tray of element cards drops onto the Isotope Scale, and atoms with the same identity begin weighing differently.
- **Isotope Scale → Periodic Elevator**. The scale flashes an atomic number, then sends the atom upward into the Periodic Elevator to reveal what its position predicts about electrons.
- **Periodic Elevator → Octopus Valence Chandelier**. The elevator opens directly beneath the Octopus Valence Chandelier, whose empty arms make the outer-shell pattern impossible to ignore.
- **Octopus Valence Chandelier → Electronegativity Magnet Wall**. One unfinished arm grabs an electron rope and yanks you toward the Electronegativity Magnet Wall.
- **Electronegativity Magnet Wall → Covalent Tug-of-War Table**. The unequal pull snaps the rope onto the Covalent Tug-of-War Table, where equal and unequal sharing become visible side by side.
- **Covalent Tug-of-War Table → Ion Coat Check**. One contestant finally takes the electron completely. An alarm sends both newly charged atoms racing to the Ion Coat Check.
- **Ion Coat Check → Hydrogen-Bond Bridge**. The opposite charges lock together, but a weaker attraction begins flickering across the next corridor: the Hydrogen-Bond Bridge.
- **Hydrogen-Bond Bridge → Macromolecule Element Crates**. The bridge leads into the biological wing, where crates of C, H, and O are already being unloaded beside the Macromolecule Element Crates.
- **Macromolecule Element Crates → Element-Role Workbench**. The crates are incomplete without several specialized element roles, so Mina wheels them to the Element-Role Workbench.

**Payoff.** The Hydrogen-Bond Bridge hums, the macromolecule crates lock into their proper bays, and Mina restores the main lights. The museum is quiet again, but the atomic rules now feel like places you have actually visited.

### Z3 — The Flooded Sky Hotel

**Tagline.** The hotel’s water system is behaving impossibly. Reach the rooftop reservoir before the building overheats.

**Premise.** You step into a luxury hotel built around a giant indoor atrium. A pipe bursts. Water clings to walls, climbs narrow tubes, resists heating, cools rooms as it evaporates, and freezes into floating platforms. None of it behaves like ordinary decoration.

**Mission.** Follow the escaping water upward through the atrium, understand each strange behavior, and reach the rooftop control gate before the hotel’s thermal system fails.

**Guide.** Mara, the hotel engineer

**Route.** Life Fountain → Polar Waterfall → Cohesion Pool → Xylem Tower → Thermal Bath → Evaporation Balcony → Ice Lake → Solution Bar → Hydration Aquarium → pH Staircase → Buffer Gate.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Life Fountain → Polar Waterfall**. The burst pipe throws a spray across the lobby and the droplets twist unevenly as they fall toward the Polar Waterfall.
- **Polar Waterfall → Cohesion Pool**. Those partial charges make neighboring molecules tug at one another, dragging the flood toward the Cohesion Pool.
- **Cohesion Pool → Xylem Tower**. The surface stretches and droplets cling to the walls. A narrow emergency tube above the pool begins pulling the water upward into Xylem Tower.
- **Xylem Tower → Thermal Bath**. The rising column reaches the hotel heating pipes, where the water is forced into the Thermal Bath.
- **Thermal Bath → Evaporation Balcony**. The bath absorbs so much heat that steam begins escaping toward the Evaporation Balcony.
- **Evaporation Balcony → Ice Lake**. Evaporation cools the balcony, but the escaping water condenses and freezes farther down the service corridor at Ice Lake.
- **Ice Lake → Solution Bar**. The floating ice exposes a service hatch beneath it. The hatch opens directly behind the Solution Bar.
- **Solution Bar → Hydration Aquarium**. A dissolved salt order spills into a glass tunnel, and the ions become surrounded inside the Hydration Aquarium.
- **Hydration Aquarium → pH Staircase**. The aquarium drain splits into acidic and basic channels that climb the pH Staircase.
- **pH Staircase → Buffer Gate**. At the top, the pH alarm starts swinging too far in both directions. The only way out is through the Buffer Gate.

**Payoff.** At the Buffer Gate, the final chemical alarm steadies. Mara closes the emergency valve. Looking back down through the atrium, every earlier water behavior now belongs to the same molecule and the same web of hydrogen-bond-driven properties.

### Z4 — The Carbon Shape-Shifter’s Workshop

**Tagline.** A master builder can make almost anything from one four-handed atom.

**Premise.** Inside the workshop, carbon skeletons keep changing shape faster than the inventory system can label them. Chains branch, bonds multiply, and small functional attachments completely change what the structures can do.

**Mission.** Help Caro rebuild the workshop catalog by identifying why carbon is so versatile and how functional groups change molecular behavior.

**Guide.** Caro, the workshop foreman

**Route.** Organic Signboard → Carbon Workbench → Functional-Group Pegboard.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Organic Signboard → Carbon Workbench**. A carbon model rolls off the signboard and lands on the Carbon Workbench, where four mechanical hands immediately start building.
- **Carbon Workbench → Functional-Group Pegboard**. One finished carbon skeleton is chemically bland until Caro snaps a small attachment onto it and sends you to the Functional-Group Pegboard.

**Payoff.** Caro hangs the final functional group back on the pegboard. The workshop stops shape-shifting at random. You now have the central carbon rule, the skeleton possibilities, and the functional-group idea attached to one compact route.

### Z5 — The Molecular Assembly Line Jam

**Tagline.** The macromolecule factory is stuck halfway between building and breaking.

**Premise.** A giant conveyor is carrying monomers into the Polymer Assembly Hall, but the line has jammed. One station keeps welding units together while another keeps washing them apart.

**Mission.** Get the line moving again by sorting monomers and polymers, controlling dehydration synthesis and hydrolysis, and shipping the four macromolecule classes to the correct bays.

**Guide.** Pip, the assembly-hall mechanic

**Route.** Macromolecule Receiving Dock → Dehydration Welder → Hydrolysis Wash Station → Comparison Shipping Board.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Macromolecule Receiving Dock → Dehydration Welder**. A loose monomer rolls off a crate and jams the Dehydration Welder, forcing the line to show exactly how smaller units become larger ones.
- **Dehydration Welder → Hydrolysis Wash Station**. The newly built polymer reaches the next station, where a spray of water activates the Hydrolysis Wash Station and reverses the process.
- **Hydrolysis Wash Station → Comparison Shipping Board**. With building and breaking restored, the products finally reach the Comparison Shipping Board for one last class-by-class check.

**Payoff.** The last crate rolls onto the Comparison Shipping Board. Pip throws the master switch, and the whole hall finally moves in one direction. Building and breaking now feel like opposite operations on the same assembly line.

### Z6 — The Greenhouse Sugar Heist

**Tagline.** Someone has swapped the greenhouse’s energy stores and structural materials.

**Premise.** The Carbohydrate Greenhouse opens to chaos. Sweet seedlings are in the wrong beds, storage jars are mixed with structural fibers, and a suspicious alpha/beta path keeps redirecting the vines.

**Mission.** Follow the sugar trail from single units to larger carbohydrates, recover the storage molecules, rebuild the structural walls, and discover how some animals gain access to cellulose with microbial help.

**Guide.** Suki, the greenhouse keeper

**Route.** Mono-Sugar Seedling → Sucrose Trellis → Polysaccharide Vine → Storage Pantry Alcove → Structural Wall Alcove → Glycosidic Archway → Alpha/Beta Path → Cellulose Fiber Barn.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Mono-Sugar Seedling → Sucrose Trellis**. A sugar seedling grows toward the Sucrose Trellis, where single units begin pairing into larger carbohydrates.
- **Sucrose Trellis → Polysaccharide Vine**. The paired sugars feed a vine that refuses to stop growing, pulling you into the Polysaccharide Vine.
- **Polysaccharide Vine → Storage Pantry Alcove**. The long chains split into two corridors: one leads to the Storage Pantry Alcove and the other will eventually become structural material.
- **Storage Pantry Alcove → Structural Wall Alcove**. A burst storage jar sends sugar chains across the hall and into the Structural Wall Alcove, where similar units serve a completely different role.
- **Structural Wall Alcove → Glycosidic Archway**. A loose cellulose panel swings open and reveals the Glycosidic Archway holding sugar units together.
- **Glycosidic Archway → Alpha/Beta Path**. The arch forks at an alpha/beta marker, forcing you onto the Alpha/Beta Path to see how a small orientation difference changes the polymer.
- **Alpha/Beta Path → Cellulose Fiber Barn**. The cellulose path ends at a barn full of plant fiber that the animals cannot digest alone. The final clue is inside the Cellulose Fiber Barn.

**Payoff.** At the Cellulose Fiber Barn, the missing feed labels are recovered and the symbiotic microbes return to work. Suki closes the greenhouse ledger. One sugar route now connects small building blocks, storage, structure, linkage, and digestion.

### Z7 — The Protein Tailor’s Emergency

**Tagline.** A famous client’s protein has unfolded minutes before delivery. Rebuild it before the shipment leaves.

**Premise.** The Protein Factory is in emergency mode. A finished protein has lost its shape, and the client is waiting. Milo gives you the raw amino-acid pieces and tells you there is no shortcut: sequence, chemistry, folding, and assembly all have to work.

**Mission.** Rebuild the protein from amino acids, connect the chain correctly, follow the folding levels, and protect the finished structure from the denaturation furnace.

**Guide.** Milo, the master protein tailor

**Route.** Protein Entrance → Amino-Acid Workbench → R-Group Sorting Tank → Peptide-Tie Station → N/C Conveyor → Sequence Folding Machine → Function Showroom → Four-Level Elevator → Secondary Folding Room → Tertiary Interaction Chamber → Quaternary Assembly Bay → Raw Chain Inspection → Soluble-Protein Water Tank → Denaturation Furnace.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Protein Entrance → Amino-Acid Workbench**. Milo places the damaged protein on a cart and sends you to the Amino-Acid Workbench to rebuild it from the smallest useful pieces.
- **Amino-Acid Workbench → R-Group Sorting Tank**. The variable side chain on the amino-acid frame keeps changing, so Milo drops the pieces into the R-Group Sorting Tank.
- **R-Group Sorting Tank → Peptide-Tie Station**. Once the side-chain behaviors are sorted, the amino acids reach the Peptide-Tie Station and begin joining into a chain.
- **Peptide-Tie Station → N/C Conveyor**. The growing chain leaves the clamp and lands on the N/C Conveyor, which forces you to track its two different ends.
- **N/C Conveyor → Sequence Folding Machine**. The directional chain feeds directly into the Sequence Folding Machine, where its order begins controlling what shape can form.
- **Sequence Folding Machine → Function Showroom**. The first stable fold lights a delivery ticket and sends the molecule into the Function Showroom to prove that shape has consequences.
- **Function Showroom → Four-Level Elevator**. The showroom elevator refuses to certify the protein until you inspect every structural level, so Milo sends you into the Four-Level Elevator.
- **Four-Level Elevator → Secondary Folding Room**. The elevator stops first at the Secondary Folding Room, where local backbone interactions create recognizable patterns.
- **Secondary Folding Room → Tertiary Interaction Chamber**. Those local folds are only part of the job. The chain is pulled into the Tertiary Interaction Chamber for whole-chain folding.
- **Tertiary Interaction Chamber → Quaternary Assembly Bay**. When one folded chain is not enough, a loading door opens to the Quaternary Assembly Bay and multiple subunits arrive.
- **Quaternary Assembly Bay → Raw Chain Inspection**. The assembled protein passes inspection, but Milo deliberately places an unfinished chain beside it at Raw Chain Inspection.
- **Raw Chain Inspection → Soluble-Protein Water Tank**. To see why environment matters, the raw and folded chains are lowered into the Soluble-Protein Water Tank.
- **Soluble-Protein Water Tank → Denaturation Furnace**. A temperature alarm suddenly sounds. The protein is rushed toward the Denaturation Furnace for the final stress test.

**Payoff.** The rebuilt protein reaches the Function Showroom, folds correctly, and switches on its function lamp. Milo seals the package just before the loading door closes. The entire protein story now runs from sequence to structure to function.

### Z8 — The Lost Genetic Manuscript

**Tagline.** A damaged archive has scattered the pieces of a genetic message.

**Premise.** A storm has damaged the Nucleic Acid Archive. Pages of a genetic manuscript have come apart into sugars, bases, phosphates, and paired strands. The archive will not reopen until the message is reconstructed in the correct direction.

**Mission.** Rebuild the manuscript from nucleotide parts, restore strand direction and pairing, and carry the recovered sequence safely through the melting room.

**Guide.** Nia, the archive keeper

**Route.** DNA/RNA Front Desk → Nucleotide Pass Counter → Ring Reading Room → DNA/RNA Sugar Gallery → Backbone Bridge → 3′ Growth Desk → Sequence Catalog → Antiparallel Spiral Staircase → Base-Pair Reading Table → DNA Melting Room.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **DNA/RNA Front Desk → Nucleotide Pass Counter**. One torn archive page slides under the Nucleotide Pass Counter, where its molecular pieces must be checked before reconstruction can begin.
- **Nucleotide Pass Counter → Ring Reading Room**. The recovered bases are still unsorted. Nia carries them into the Ring Reading Room and lays them out by ring structure.
- **Ring Reading Room → DNA/RNA Sugar Gallery**. The sorted base cards are useless without the correct sugars, so the archive cart moves into the DNA/RNA Sugar Gallery.
- **DNA/RNA Sugar Gallery → Backbone Bridge**. With DNA and RNA components separated, the pieces reach the Backbone Bridge and are connected into directional strands.
- **Backbone Bridge → 3′ Growth Desk**. The strand is incomplete. A blinking arrow points toward the 3′ Growth Desk, where only one end can accept the next nucleotide.
- **3′ Growth Desk → Sequence Catalog**. As the strand lengthens, the order of bases begins forming a message. Nia carries it straight to the Sequence Catalog.
- **Sequence Catalog → Antiparallel Spiral Staircase**. The catalog can read the message, but it cannot protect it until a complementary strand is rebuilt on the Antiparallel Spiral Staircase.
- **Antiparallel Spiral Staircase → Base-Pair Reading Table**. The two opposite-running strands twist together and descend to the Base-Pair Reading Table for pairing inspection.
- **Base-Pair Reading Table → DNA Melting Room**. The paired manuscript passes every base check, then enters the DNA Melting Room for one final test of strand stability.

**Payoff.** The repaired double helix locks into the archive case and the sequence catalog comes back online. Nia turns the key. You can now retrace how components become strands, how strands pair, and how sequence carries information.

### Z9 — The Membrane Rescue Wing

**Tagline.** A living-cell exhibit is losing its membrane as the temperature swings.

**Premise.** The Lipid Membrane Wing houses a living-cell exhibit. Tonight its membrane is failing: lipid pieces are scattered, fatty-acid tails are packing incorrectly, and the temperature control keeps swinging.

**Mission.** Rebuild the lipid components, assemble a phospholipid bilayer, identify the steroid framework, and use cholesterol to stabilize the membrane across temperature changes.

**Guide.** Lena, the membrane-wing technician

**Route.** Lipid Lobby → Glycerol Hook Rack → Ester-Linkage Bench → Fatty-Acid Tail Runway → Fat Storage Vault → Phospholipid Pool Wall → Steroid Ring Gallery → Cholesterol Thermostat Gate.

**Causal scene movement.**
- **Lipid Lobby → Glycerol Hook Rack**. One oily molecule rolls away from a water spill and lands beneath the Glycerol Hook Rack, where the first lipid components are waiting.
- **Glycerol Hook Rack → Ester-Linkage Bench**. Three fatty-acid tails swing from the rack and are carried to the Ester-Linkage Bench to be connected covalently.
- **Ester-Linkage Bench → Fatty-Acid Tail Runway**. The finished lipid model rolls onto the Fatty-Acid Tail Runway, where straight and kinked tails pack very differently.
- **Fatty-Acid Tail Runway → Fat Storage Vault**. The runway ends at a storage door. The fat molecules disappear into the Fat Storage Vault, revealing why organisms keep them.
- **Fat Storage Vault → Phospholipid Pool Wall**. A membrane alarm interrupts the tour. Lena pulls out phospholipids and rushes you to the Phospholipid Pool Wall.
- **Phospholipid Pool Wall → Steroid Ring Gallery**. The bilayer stabilizes, but a rigid four-ring molecule is still unidentified. The next clue waits in the Steroid Ring Gallery.
- **Steroid Ring Gallery → Cholesterol Thermostat Gate**. One steroid card is marked cholesterol and points toward the temperature controls at the Cholesterol Thermostat Gate.

**Payoff.** The final cholesterol molecules settle into the membrane and the thermostat stops oscillating. Lena watches the cell recover. The wing now tells one connected story about hydrophobic molecules, fat structure, bilayers, steroids, and membrane fluidity.
