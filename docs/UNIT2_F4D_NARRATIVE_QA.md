# Unit 2 F4D Narrative QA

## Release scope

F4D adds only Unit 2 Journey 4, **Membrane Border Terminal**. Unit 2 remains a developer preview and is not student-released.

## Narrative result

- Story title: **The Border Terminal Breach**
- 8 connected scenes
- 21 locked Unit 2 knowledge records
- 3 optional first-exposure Quick Recalls
- 3,167 narrative words
- Average scene length: 395.9 words
- Shortest scene: 334 words

## Narrative quality controls

Every scene has a fixed left / center / right layout, Dr. Nia Park as the stable guide, at least three clearly identified scientific parts or continuity objects, explicit visual identity and job descriptions, a visible biological action before terminology is named, a causal transition into the next locus, conventional-science visual requirements, and preserved misconception guards.

The continuity object is one test-traveler tray containing O₂, CO₂, H₂O, NH₃, glucose, Na⁺, and K⁺. The tray is reused from the bilayer entrance through selective permeability and the molecule passport lanes so the learner compares the same molecules under changing membrane rules.

## Required distinctions explicitly taught

- amphipathic phospholipid structure and bilayer orientation
- fluid mosaic composition and lateral movement
- unsaturated-tail effects on packing
- cholesterol as a temperature-dependent fluidity buffer
- integral vs. transmembrane vs. peripheral membrane proteins
- hydrophobic vs. hydrophilic protein regions
- glycolipid vs. glycoprotein
- selective permeability as an emergent molecular property, not intentional choice
- small nonpolar vs. small uncharged polar vs. ion / large-polar passage
- cell wall vs. plasma membrane vs. plasmodesma

## Misconception safeguards

The story explicitly avoids or corrects these errors.

- Phospholipids are not depicted as permanently fixed in place.
- Routine phospholipid movement is lateral, not continual leaflet-to-leaflet flip-flop.
- Cholesterol is not presented as always increasing or always decreasing fluidity.
- Integral and transmembrane are not treated as synonyms.
- Peripheral proteins do not enter the bilayer hydrophobic core.
- Water is not described as completely unable to cross the lipid bilayer.
- Selective permeability is not attributed to an intentional membrane gatekeeper.
- The plant cell wall is not presented as the primary selective boundary into cytoplasm.
- Plants are not presented as the only organisms with cell walls.
