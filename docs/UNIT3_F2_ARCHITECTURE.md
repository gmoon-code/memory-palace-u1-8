## Journey architecture

### U3-J1 — Enzyme Catalysis Workshop
Catalysts, activation energy, active-site compatibility, induced fit, and enzyme reuse

Setting logic: A molecular workshop makes the reaction barrier, docking geometry, and catalytic turnover visible before terminology is attached.

- **Catalysis and substrate binding** — Catalyst Energy Ramp → Active-Site Dock → Catalytic Turnover Bench

### U3-J2 — Enzyme Regulation Control Wing
Environmental effects, enzyme concentration variables, helpers, inhibition, allostery, cooperativity, and pathway regulation

Setting logic: A control wing allows one enzyme system to be stressed, supplied, blocked, activated, and regulated while preserving clear distinctions among mechanisms.

- **Environmental and concentration effects** — Enzyme Stability Chamber → Collision and Saturation Track
- **Helpers and inhibitor mechanisms** — Cofactor Assembly Station → Competitive Inhibitor Gate → Allosteric Control Panel → Cooperative Binding Array
- **Pathway-level regulation** — Feedback Loop Control Room → Irreversible Inhibition Lockout

### U3-J3 — Cellular Energy Exchange Hall
Metabolism, thermodynamics, free energy, cellular work, ATP structure, coupling, and regeneration

Setting logic: An energy-accounting hall keeps forms of energy, thermodynamic constraints, work, and ATP cycling inside one causal system instead of treating them as separate definitions.

- **Metabolism and energy forms** — Metabolism Route Map → Energy Forms Gallery
- **Thermodynamic constraints** — First-Law Ledger → Entropy and Living Order Chamber → Free-Energy Terrain
- **Cellular work and ATP coupling** — Cellular Work Dock → ATP Structure Station → ATP Hydrolysis Forge → ATP Regeneration Wheel
- **Evolutionary conservation of metabolism** — Conserved Metabolism Archive

### U3-J4 — Light Capture Conservatory
Photosynthetic origins, chloroplast geography, light, pigments, photosystems, electron transport, proton gradients, and photophosphorylation

Setting logic: A light-processing conservatory follows energy from incoming photons through pigments, photosystems, electron transfer, proton-gradient formation, ATP, and NADPH.

- **Photosynthetic context and origins** — Carbon Strategy Entrance → Oxygenation Timeline
- **Leaf and chloroplast organization** — Leaf Gas-Exchange Balcony → Chloroplast Compartment Gallery → Photosynthesis Redox Board
- **Light and photosystem capture** — Photon and Wavelength Prism → Pigment Spectrum Bench → Photosystem Antenna Theater
- **Linear electron flow and energy products** — Photosystem II Water Splitter → Thylakoid Electron-Transport Bridge → Photosystem I NADPH Station → Photophosphorylation Turbine

### U3-J5 — Carbon Fixation Greenhouse
Calvin cycle, carbon fixation, Rubisco, RuBP, G3P, photorespiration, C4, and CAM

Setting logic: A carbon greenhouse turns ATP and NADPH into visible carbon-flow work, then tests how plants reduce photorespiration under different environmental constraints.

- **Calvin-cycle carbon flow** — Calvin Cycle Energy Dock → Carbon Fixation Bench → G3P Output and Regeneration Loop
- **Photorespiration and carbon-concentration strategies** — Photorespiration Detour → C4 and CAM Strategy Conservatory

### U3-J6 — Respiration Power Plant
Aerobic respiration from fuel intake through glycolysis, mitochondrial oxidation, electron transport, proton gradients, ATP synthase, heat, and prokaryotic respiration

Setting logic: A power plant follows carbon and electrons through successive energy-harvesting stages while keeping mitochondrial compartments and chemiosmosis spatially explicit.

- **Fuel entry and glycolysis** — Respiration Fuel Intake → Glycolysis Floor → Electron Carrier Charging Bay
- **Mitochondrial carbon oxidation** — Pyruvate Oxidation Airlock → Citric Acid Cycle Chamber → Mitochondrial Architecture Bay
- **Electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation** — Respiratory Electron-Transport Descent → Proton Pumping Wall → Oxygen Terminal-Acceptor Basin → Oxidative-Phosphorylation Turbine
- **Heat and cross-domain respiration** — Uncoupling Heat Vent → Prokaryotic Respiratory Membrane

### U3-J7 — Alternative Energy Annex
Aerobic versus anaerobic respiration, fermentation, NAD+ recycling, alcohol fermentation, and lactate metabolism

Setting logic: A branching annex compares alternative routes from shared metabolic constraints so anaerobic respiration and fermentation cannot collapse into the same idea.

- **Alternative electron and redox routes** — Respiration Across Life Junction → Fermentation NAD+ Recycling Gate
- **Fermentation products and physiological context** — Alcohol Fermentation Vat → Lactate Fermentation Track
