## Retrieval and science-bearing targets

### 1. Metabolism Route Map

- **Sequential metabolic pathways** — Energy-related pathways are sequential, allowing controlled transfer of energy through a series of reactions.
- **Pathway intermediates** — A product of one reaction in a metabolic pathway commonly serves as a reactant for the next reaction.
- **Metabolism** — Metabolism is the complete set of chemical reactions occurring within an organism or cell.
- **Metabolic pathway** — A metabolic pathway is a sequence of enzyme-catalyzed reactions in which intermediates connect an initial substrate to products.
- **Catabolic pathway** — Catabolic pathways break complex molecules into simpler products and typically release usable energy.
- **Anabolic pathway** — Anabolic pathways build complex molecules from simpler components and require energy input.
- **Enzymes in catabolic reactions** — Enzymes can catalyze reactions involved in catabolic breakdown pathways.
- **Enzymes in anabolic reactions** — Enzymes can catalyze reactions involved in anabolic biosynthetic pathways.
- Optional Quick Recall — What distinguishes the two branches on the metabolic route map?

### 2. Energy Forms Gallery

- **Energy** — Energy is the capacity to cause change or perform work in a biological system.
- **Kinetic energy** — Kinetic energy is energy associated with motion.
- **Thermal energy** — Thermal energy is kinetic energy associated with random movement of atoms and molecules.
- **Potential energy** — Potential energy is stored energy associated with position or structure.
- **Chemical energy** — Chemical energy is potential energy associated with molecular structure that can be transformed during chemical reactions.

### 3. First-Law Ledger

- **Life and thermodynamics** — The high degree of order in living systems is consistent with the first and second laws of thermodynamics.
- **Thermodynamics** — Thermodynamics is the study of energy transformations in matter.
- **First law of thermodynamics** — Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can be transferred or transformed.

### 4. Entropy and Living Order Chamber

- **Energy input and maintenance** — Energy input must exceed energy loss to maintain biological order and power cellular processes.
- **Loss of energy flow** — A significant loss of biological order or energy flow results in death.
- **Second law of thermodynamics** — Every energy transfer or transformation increases the entropy of the universe, and some energy becomes less available to perform work, often dispersing as heat.
- **Entropy and living order** — A cell or organism can become more ordered locally while total entropy of the system plus surroundings increases.

### 5. Free-Energy Terrain

- **Gibbs free energy** — Gibbs free energy describes the portion of a system’s energy that can perform work under specified conditions.
- **Exergonic reaction** — An exergonic reaction has a negative change in free energy and can proceed spontaneously in the thermodynamic sense; spontaneity does not imply a fast rate.
- **Endergonic reaction** — An endergonic reaction has a positive change in free energy and requires coupling to an energy-releasing process to proceed in cells.
- **Cells remain out of equilibrium** — Living cells maintain flows of matter and energy and remain far from equilibrium; reaching equilibrium is incompatible with sustained cellular work.
- Optional Quick Recall — Which direction on the terrain is thermodynamically favorable without added energy?

### 6. Cellular Work Dock

- **Energy input** — All living systems require an input of energy.
- **Energy coupling** — Cellular processes that release energy can be coupled to processes that require energy.
- **Mechanical cellular work** — Mechanical work includes processes such as ciliary movement, chromosome movement, and muscle contraction.
- **Transport work** — Transport work moves substances across membranes against thermodynamically favored directions.
- **Chemical work** — Chemical work drives endergonic reactions such as synthesis of polymers from monomers.

### 7. ATP Structure Station

- **ATP** — Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is a nucleotide used as an immediate energy-coupling molecule for many forms of cellular work.
- **ATP structure** — ATP contains adenine, ribose, and three phosphate groups.

### 8. ATP Hydrolysis Forge

- **ATP hydrolysis** — ATP hydrolysis converts ATP and water to ADP and inorganic phosphate and has a negative free-energy change under cellular conditions.
- **ATP hydrolysis energy source** — Energy released by ATP hydrolysis comes from the overall chemical change to lower-free-energy products, not from energy released by breaking a phosphate bond itself.
- **ATP energy coupling** — Cells couple exergonic ATP hydrolysis to endergonic cellular processes so the combined process can proceed.
- **Phosphorylation** — Phosphorylation is transfer or addition of a phosphate group to a molecule; ATP-driven phosphorylation can alter a molecule’s reactivity, free energy, or protein conformation.

### 9. ATP Regeneration Wheel

- **ATP regeneration cycle** — ATP is continually regenerated from ADP and inorganic phosphate using energy released by exergonic processes.

### 10. Conserved Metabolism Archive

- **Conserved core metabolism** — Core metabolic pathways such as glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation are conserved across Archaea, Bacteria, and Eukarya, supporting common ancestry.
