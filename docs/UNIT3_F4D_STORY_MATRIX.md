# Unit 3 F4D Story Matrix

## Journey 4 · Light Capture Conservatory

**Story:** The Light That Could Not Reach the Greenhouse

**Premise:** A light-capture conservatory is receiving sunlight but cannot reliably convert it into ATP and NADPH. The learner follows light from ecological context into leaf and chloroplast structure, pigments, photosystems, electron transfer, proton-gradient formation, and photophosphorylation.

**Mission:** Build one continuous map from photon capture to linear electron flow and the chemical energy products that leave the light reactions.

The same route is preserved from the locked F3 architecture. Light begins as a real photon input. After absorption, the narrative changes the continuity marker to excitation energy. Only at the reaction center does it begin highlighting an actual transferred electron. A separate proton-gradient gauge rises across the thylakoid route.

## Scene-by-scene science anchors

### 1. Carbon Strategy Entrance

**Narrative problem.** The conservatory cannot even begin its light route until carbon source and energy source stop being treated as the same question.

**Terms and relationships.** Photosynthesis overall process; Solar energy capture; Autotroph; Photoautotroph; Heterotroph

**Transition.** You follow the pulsing oxygen line into the next gallery, where the conservatory’s history is laid out as a timeline rather than a reaction pathway.

### 2. Oxygenation Timeline

**Narrative problem.** Before chloroplasts appear, the light route begins in prokaryotic life and changes the planet around it.

**Terms and relationships.** Prokaryotic origin of photosynthesis; Atmospheric oxygenation; Foundation of eukaryotic photosynthesis; Cyanobacteria and oxygenic photosynthesis

**Transition.** The leaf silhouette becomes a real suspended leaf ahead of you, and a tiny pore on its lower surface expands until it is large enough to walk toward.

### 3. Leaf Gas-Exchange Balcony

**Narrative problem.** Light capture inside the leaf depends on a route for carbon dioxide to reach photosynthetic tissue while other gases can leave.

**Terms and relationships.** Leaf photosynthesis; Stomata

**Transition.** You pass through the enlarged chloroplast envelope and enter a cutaway gallery where stroma and thylakoids remain visible at the same time.

### 4. Chloroplast Compartment Gallery

**Narrative problem.** The light route fails if the thylakoid membrane and stroma are treated as interchangeable places.

**Terms and relationships.** Chloroplast compartments; Stroma and carbon fixation; Thylakoid membrane machinery; Grana and light reactions; Chloroplast

**Transition.** The chloroplast cutaway remains behind you as a reference map while water enters the left side of the next redox board and carbon dioxide enters the right.

### 5. Photosynthesis Redox Board

**Narrative problem.** The overall photosynthesis equation hides two different redox stories, so the board pulls them apart before reconnecting them.

**Terms and relationships.** Photosynthesis equation as model; Photosynthetic oxygen comes from water; Photosynthesis as redox; Light reactions and Calvin cycle

**Transition.** A narrow beam of white light leaves the redox board and enters a glass prism, while the electron and proton parts of the mechanism remain deliberately unresolved.

### 6. Photon and Wavelength Prism

**Narrative problem.** Before a pigment can capture light, the conservatory has to distinguish photon energy from wavelength and color.

**Terms and relationships.** Photon; Wavelength; Pigment absorption and reflection

**Transition.** With the original photon now absorbed, you follow the excitation indicator into the Pigment Spectrum Bench rather than carrying a fictional light particle forward.

### 7. Pigment Spectrum Bench

**Narrative problem.** The same spectrum reaches several pigments, but they do not absorb, excite, or protect the photosynthetic system in exactly the same way.

**Terms and relationships.** Light excitation of chlorophyll; Chlorophyll; Chlorophyll a; Chlorophyll b; Carotenoids

**Transition.** The pigment panels fold inward into a complete photosystem, and the excitation indicator begins moving toward the center without any electron yet leaving one antenna pigment for another.

### 8. Photosystem Antenna Theater

**Narrative problem.** The conservatory’s most dangerous shortcut is exposed here: excitation can move through an antenna without one electron hopping from pigment to pigment.

**Terms and relationships.** Photosystem; Reaction center; Light-harvesting complex; Excitation-energy transfer

**Transition.** You follow the newly highlighted electron path into photosystem II, while the antenna theater remains behind as the place where excitation energy and electron transfer were permanently separated.

### 9. Photosystem II Water Splitter

**Narrative problem.** Photosystem II can send an excited electron onward only if the reaction center is supplied with a replacement, and water provides it.

**Terms and relationships.** Water supplies PSII electrons; Photosystem II; Water-splitting products

**Transition.** The oxygen outlet vents away, the lumen keeps its new protons, and you follow the highlighted electron onto the thylakoid electron-transport bridge.

### 10. Thylakoid Electron-Transport Bridge

**Narrative problem.** The electron does not become ATP; its transfer through membrane machinery helps establish the proton gradient that will later power ATP synthase.

**Terms and relationships.** ETC locations across life; Photosystem linkage; Thylakoid proton gradient

**Transition.** The electron arrives at photosystem I and pauses beside a second light receiver, while the elevated lumen H+ gauge remains behind as a separate product of the bridge.

### 11. Photosystem I NADPH Station

**Narrative problem.** Photosystem I raises electron energy again and directs reducing power into NADPH on the stroma side.

**Terms and relationships.** NADPH formation; Photosystem I; P680 and P700 reaction-center labels

**Transition.** NADPH waits at the stroma-side exit while you follow the still-unresolved proton gradient to ATP synthase for the final light-reaction step.

### 12. Photophosphorylation Turbine

**Narrative problem.** The final conversion becomes clear only when the learner sees that H+ movement through ATP synthase directly drives ATP formation.

**Terms and relationships.** Light-reaction energy products; Photophosphorylation; Light-reaction inputs and outputs

**Transition.** ATP and NADPH leave together through the stroma-side greenhouse door, completing the Light Capture Conservatory and setting up the next journey through carbon fixation.
