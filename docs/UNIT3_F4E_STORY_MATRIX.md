# Unit 3 F4E Story Matrix

## Journey 5 · Carbon Fixation Greenhouse

**Story:** The Greenhouse That Kept Losing Carbon

**Premise:** The carbon-fixation greenhouse has ATP and NADPH available but carbon output falls whenever conditions favor photorespiration. The learner reconstructs the Calvin cycle and then compares two carbon-concentrating strategies.

**Mission:** Follow carbon from CO₂ fixation through G3P output and RuBP regeneration, then diagnose rubisco oxygenation and compare C4 spatial separation with CAM temporal separation.

The continuity object is one amber carbon tracer. It enters as the carbon atom in CO₂, becomes fixed organic carbon, contributes to G3P output, and is then contrasted with the photorespiratory detour. ATP and NADPH remain separate energy/reducing-power inputs and are never portrayed as carbon sources.

## Scene-by-scene science anchors

### 1. Calvin Cycle Energy Dock

**Narrative problem.** ATP and NADPH have reached the stroma, but the greenhouse still has no carbon output because energy products and carbon source are different inputs.

**Terms and relationships.** Calvin-cycle energy input; Calvin cycle

**Transition.** One amber carbon tracer enters as CO₂ and is physically carried to the Carbon Fixation Bench.

### 2. Carbon Fixation Bench

**Narrative problem.** The learner must keep incoming CO₂, the rubisco enzyme, and the RuBP acceptor from collapsing into one vague “carbon-fixation molecule.”

**Terms and relationships.** Carbon fixation; Rubisco; RuBP

**Transition.** The newly fixed carbon enters the cycle and moves toward the G3P/output versus RuBP/regeneration loop.

### 3. G3P Output and Regeneration Loop

**Narrative problem.** Carbon processing has two obligations: produce net organic-carbon output and regenerate the RuBP acceptor so another CO₂ molecule can enter.

**Terms and relationships.** G3P; detailed Calvin-cycle stoichiometry

**Scope handling.** The three-CO₂ model shows 9 ATP, 6 NADPH, one net G3P, and five G3P equivalents contributing to regeneration of three RuBP. The accounting is deliberately subordinate to the larger mechanism and does not become a requirement to memorize every Calvin-cycle intermediate.

**Transition.** Hot/dry conditions reduce internal CO₂ availability relative to O₂ and expose rubisco’s competing oxygenation route.

### 4. Photorespiration Detour

**Narrative problem.** The same rubisco enzyme can react with O₂ instead of CO₂, lowering net carbon fixation and consuming energy.

**Terms and relationships.** Photorespiration; photorespiration evolutionary interpretation

**Guard.** Photorespiration is physically separated from later mitochondrial cellular respiration. The evolutionary-legacy explanation remains a qualified hypothesis, and possible protective roles remain visible.

**Transition.** The exact hot/dry pressure that exposed photorespiration is carried into two comparison greenhouses.

### 5. C4 and CAM Strategy Conservatory

**Narrative problem.** Two plant strategies address the same photorespiration/water-loss pressure through different forms of separation.

**Terms and relationships.** C4 photosynthesis; CAM photosynthesis

**Permanent discrimination.** **C4 = spatial separation. CAM = temporal separation.**

**Payoff.** C4 separates initial carbon fixation in mesophyll cells from Calvin-cycle processing in bundle-sheath cells. CAM opens stomata mainly at night, stores fixed carbon in organic acids, and releases CO₂ during the day for the Calvin cycle. The greenhouse restores net carbon gain, and organic carbon is ready to enter the next journey through cellular respiration.
