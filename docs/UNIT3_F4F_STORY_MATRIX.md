# Unit 3 F4F Story Matrix

## Journey 6 · Respiration Power Plant

**Story:** The Power Plant With Fuel but No Power

**Premise:** Fuel reaches a respiration plant, yet the ATP grid remains dark because the carbon route, electron-carrier route, proton-gradient machinery, and ATP turbine have been disconnected. Dr. Nia Park and the learner rebuild the pathway in the order the cell actually uses it.

**Mission:** Follow separate carbon and electron tracers from cytosolic glycolysis through mitochondrial carbon oxidation, respiratory electron transfer, proton pumping, oxygen reduction, chemiosmosis, ATP synthase, uncoupling, and a prokaryotic membrane comparison.

The continuity system contains two deliberately separate tracers. An **amber carbon tracer** follows organic carbon through oxidation and eventual CO₂ release. A **blue electron tracer** moves onto reduced carriers and through the respiratory ETC toward the terminal acceptor. A third red H⁺ gauge appears only when electron-transfer energy is coupled to proton pumping. The learner therefore never has to imagine carbon, electrons, protons, and ATP as one interchangeable form of “energy.”

## Scene-by-scene science anchors

### 1. Respiration Fuel Intake

The opening scene prevents cellular respiration from becoming one vague mitochondrial event. Carbohydrate-, fat-, and protein-derived fuels remain separate inputs. The central route groups aerobic eukaryotic respiration into glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation plus the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. The net glucose-respiration equation is displayed as accounting, while carbon oxidation and oxygen reduction are introduced as the underlying redox direction.

### 2. Glycolysis Floor

The entire scene stays in the **cytosol**. One six-carbon glucose becomes two three-carbon pyruvate molecules. Two ATP are invested and four are produced in the classroom bookkeeping model, giving a net gain of two. ATP formation here is identified as **substrate-level phosphorylation**, direct enzymatic transfer of phosphate from an organic substrate to ADP. No CO₂ vent opens during glycolysis.

### 3. Electron Carrier Charging Bay

NAD⁺/NADH and FAD/FADH₂ occupy fixed opposite sides. A representative blue electron tracer is loaded onto the carriers. The story explicitly states that the FAD/FADH₂ model previews later citric-cycle chemistry and does not imply that glycolysis produced FADH₂.

### 4. Pyruvate Oxidation Airlock

Three-carbon pyruvate enters mitochondrial processing. Carbon leaves as CO₂, NAD⁺ is reduced to NADH, and the remaining two-carbon acetyl group is attached to coenzyme A to form acetyl-CoA. FADH₂ is reserved for later cycle chemistry.

### 5. Citric Acid Cycle Chamber

The cycle remains in the mitochondrial matrix. Acetyl-derived carbon is oxidized, CO₂ is released, NAD⁺ and FAD are reduced, a small substrate-level ATP contribution appears, and the cycle acceptor is regenerated. Exact intermediate memorization remains secondary. The per-glucose accounting display shows 4 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂ across two turns.

### 6. Mitochondrial Architecture Bay

A conventional mitochondrial cutaway permanently separates matrix, inner membrane/cristae, and intermembrane space. Glycolysis remains outside the mitochondrion in the cytosol. Pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle are attached to the matrix. Electron transport and ATP-synthesis machinery are attached to the inner membrane. Cristae are taught as increased membrane surface for this machinery, not as an energy source themselves.

### 7. Respiratory Electron-Transport Descent

NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the respiratory ETC. Electrons pass through inner-membrane redox carriers toward a terminal acceptor while moving toward lower-free-energy states. The electron tracer never enters an ATP molecule. Aerobic respiration, anaerobic respiration, and fermentation remain distinct.

### 8. Proton Pumping Wall

Energy released by electron transfer through selected ETC complexes is coupled to proton movement from the mitochondrial matrix into the intermembrane space. The resulting gradient has higher H⁺ concentration and lower pH in the intermembrane space, while the matrix has lower H⁺ concentration and higher pH. Electron movement and proton movement remain separate animations.

### 9. Oxygen Terminal-Acceptor Basin

Oxygen accepts terminal electrons and is reduced to water. The balanced terminal model `O₂ + 4 e⁻ + 4 H⁺ → 2 H₂O` is visible. The ATP counter does not move during this reaction, preventing oxygen from being remembered as a direct ATP source.

### 10. Oxidative-Phosphorylation Turbine

The immediate energy source is the **proton-motive force**. H⁺ moves through ATP synthase by chemiosmosis. Proton flow drives rotational/conformational changes that support ATP formation from ADP and inorganic phosphate. Oxidative phosphorylation is presented as the connected system of electron transport that establishes the gradient plus chemiosmotic ATP synthesis that uses it. Approximate ATP yields remain variable, with roughly 26–28 ATP from oxidative phosphorylation and roughly 30–32 total per glucose in many eukaryotic cells used as estimates rather than a universal total.

### 11. Uncoupling Heat Vent

A proton bypass dissipates part of the gradient without ATP synthase. ATP capture falls while heat release rises. The scene explains why proton leak changes energetic efficiency and how regulated uncoupling can contribute to endotherm thermoregulation.

### 12. Prokaryotic Respiratory Membrane

The final scene removes the mitochondrion while preserving the membrane mechanism. Prokaryotic respiratory electron transport and proton translocation occur across the plasma membrane. Cytoplasm and the external/periplasmic proton side replace the mitochondrial matrix/intermembrane-space geography. The journey closes by preserving the distinction from fermentation, which is the focus of Journey 7.

## Permanent causal chain

**fuel oxidation → reduced electron carriers → respiratory ETC → proton pumping → proton-motive force → chemiosmosis through ATP synthase → ATP**

Oxygen belongs at the terminal electron-acceptor end of the electron route. Carbon leaves primarily through CO₂-producing oxidation stages. H⁺ stores membrane potential. None of those three tracer systems becomes ATP directly.
