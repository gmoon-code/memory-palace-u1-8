## Why Unit 5 uses this architecture

Unit 5 contains unusually dense process, terminology, and quantitative reasoning. F2 therefore separates stable biological knowledge from procedures that must transfer to unfamiliar problems. Meiosis, inheritance terminology, chromosome behavior, and mechanism relationships receive permanent spatial anchors. Punnett-square execution, probability calculations, pedigree inference, recombination calculations, chi-square calculations, and novel inheritance problems remain Challenge Lab functions.

## Architecture rules locked at F2

- The same chromosome set must remain physically traceable throughout the meiosis route. Homologous chromosomes and sister chromatids cannot silently swap identities between scenes.
- Meiosis I must visibly separate homologous chromosomes. Meiosis II must visibly separate sister chromatids.
- Cells after meiosis I must be represented as haploid even though their chromosomes remain duplicated.
- Crossing over must occur between nonsister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during prophase I.
- Classic 3:1 and 9:3:3:1 ratios must remain conditional outcomes under the relevant Mendelian assumptions.
- X/Y chromosome-complement examples must remain explicit inheritance models and cannot be used as synonyms for gender or as a complete account of sex development.
- Recombination frequency and physical distance cannot be treated as identical. The 50% observable recombination ceiling must remain explicit.
- Mitochondrial and plant organelle inheritance must use typical/conditional parent-of-origin language, not universal maternal claims.
- Chi-square must use categorical counts, model-based expected values, and reject/fail-to-reject language. A nonsignificant result does not prove the null.
- Every locus requires a scientific visual in addition to any later mnemonic or narrative imagery.
