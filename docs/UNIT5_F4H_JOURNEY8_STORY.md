## 1. Non-Nuclear Inheritance Dock — The Dock Where Every Inheritance Route Had Been Forced Into the Nucleus

*Non-nuclear inheritance becomes understandable when organelle genomes are physically separated from nuclear chromosomes and followed through their own transmission route.*

The final wing of the Heredity complex opens into the **Inheritance Evidence Laboratory**, and the first room makes the problem obvious before anyone speaks. On your **left**, an enormous blue-and-amber nuclear chromosome diagram covers a wall and sends every trait through a Mendelian segregation lane. On your **right**, a branching channel distributes whole organelles into model daughter cells and gametes. Dr. Imani Reyes sets the **three-compartment evidence tray** on the central bench. The inheritance-route compartment is open, while the biological-case and statistical-model compartments remain closed. She tells you that this laboratory has been making one mistake repeatedly. It has treated every inherited trait as though the relevant DNA had to ride inside a nuclear chromosome.
Imani brings the familiar **organelle inheritance model** beside the left wall. The blue and amber nuclear chromosomes move through a conventional segregation animation. Then she freezes them. In the transparent cytoplasm, glowing DNA tokens remain inside mitochondria and chloroplasts. She opens the center dock and names the broad relationship **Non-nuclear inheritance overview**. Some inherited traits can arise from genetic information outside the nuclear chromosome set because mitochondrial or chloroplast genomes can be transmitted independently of nuclear chromosomes. The word non-nuclear therefore identifies where the relevant genome resides. It does not mean the trait lacks a DNA basis, and it does not erase the role of the nucleus in the rest of the cell.
The center dock now advances one cell division. Nuclear chromosomes segregate along the left reference lane. At the same time, mitochondria and chloroplasts are distributed through the cytoplasm as organelles and organelle copies move into the daughter cells. Imani names the mechanism **Non-nuclear inheritance**. Traits encoded by mitochondrial or chloroplast DNA can show inheritance patterns that depart from standard Mendelian nuclear expectations because organelle transmission is not the same process as nuclear chromosome segregation. The real mitochondria and chloroplasts remain visible. Nothing is replaced by a mnemonic creature, and no allele tag is allowed to jump from the nucleus into an organelle merely to make the scene memorable.
On the right, the branching channel opens into four model gametes. The organelle tokens are partitioned among them with visible variation in how many copies enter each compartment. Imani labels the relationship **Organelle assortment**. Mitochondrial and chloroplast genomes do not segregate according to the nuclear chromosome Mendelian pattern displayed on the left wall. Their organelles and genome copies are partitioned through the cytoplasm into daughter cells and gametes. She keeps the nuclear reference wall lit so you can compare the mechanisms without confusing them. Nuclear chromosome segregation remains real biology. Organelle assortment is another real inheritance route operating through different cellular structures.
A technician tries to stamp NON-MENDELIAN on every object in the right channel and then remove the DNA labels. Imani stops the machine. Non-nuclear inheritance does not mean mysterious inheritance without genes. The inherited information is still DNA. The distinction concerns mitochondrial or chloroplast genomes and their transmission through cytoplasmic organelles. She places one mitochondrial DNA token and one chloroplast DNA token into the first compartment of the evidence tray. The tray now records what kind of inheritance route is being examined before any parent-of-origin pattern is discussed.
The lab lights dim except for three anchors. The nuclear chromosome reference remains on the left, the organelle genome dock remains directly ahead, and the organelle assortment channel remains on the right. Imani asks you to reconstruct the room using only those anchors. If a trait depends on DNA in a mitochondrial or chloroplast genome, its transmission can follow the distribution of those organelles through cells and gametes. That is why its inheritance does not have to match a nuclear Mendelian ratio. The first optional Quick Recall can now occur because the mechanism has already been seen and named.
A narrow animal-fertilization corridor opens beyond the right channel. Imani slides the evidence tray forward while leaving the nuclear reference diagram behind. The next question is more specific. The route cannot be answered by the phrase non-nuclear alone. You will have to follow the organelles into the zygote.

In animals, mitochondrial DNA is usually transmitted through the egg much more commonly than through sperm, so mitochondrial-DNA traits are typically maternally inherited. The parent-of-origin pattern follows the organelle contribution in this model.

## 5. Chi-Square Model Bench — The Bench That Refused to Calculate Before the Model Existed

*Chi-square goodness-of-fit begins with a defined null model and categorical counts, so the statistic has meaning only after expected proportions and observed categories are established.*

The **Chi-Square Model Bench** occupies a bright statistics room with three fixed stations. On your **left**, a null-model rack holds expected category proportions for a specified inheritance model. Directly **ahead**, the **chi-square evidence console** has four categorical bins and the formula χ² = Σ((O−E)²/E) etched across its glass cover. On your **right**, a gate admits only category-count cards while a tray of continuous measurements remains outside the lane. Imani places the statistical-model compartment of the **three-compartment evidence tray** beside the console. The console stays locked. Before a number can be calculated, the model and the type of data must be clear.
Imani selects a fixed-proportion inheritance model from the left rack. The model supplies expected proportions for the outcome categories. She does not claim that the model is true merely because it has been chosen as a null model. It is the reference explanation being evaluated. She converts the expected proportions into expected counts for the sample size and places those counts in covered slots. Only then does she introduce **Chi-square hypothesis testing**. In this workflow, observed and expected categorical counts are compared through χ² = Σ((O−E)²/E), and the resulting statistic will later be evaluated with degrees of freedom and a significance criterion before any hypothesis decision is made.
The center cover rises one section. Imani names the **Chi-square goodness-of-fit test**. Its question is whether the observed categorical counts differ from the model-based expected counts by more than would be expected from sampling variation under the null hypothesis. She points to the phrase under the null hypothesis on the model rack. The procedure does not ask whether the observed data are perfectly identical to expectation. Random samples commonly deviate from exact expected proportions. The statistical test evaluates how large the deviations are relative to the model and expected category sizes.
On the right, the data-type gate tests several inputs. Cards labeled purple offspring, white offspring, recombinant class, parental class, and phenotype category pass through because they can be represented as category frequencies. A card containing individual plant heights remains outside. Imani names the rule **Chi-square uses categorical counts**. The Unit 5 goodness-of-fit procedure works with categorical counts or frequencies. Continuous measurements are not entered directly into this particular workflow as though they were category counts. A separate statistical method might be appropriate for a different data structure, but that question is outside this palace scene.
A technician tries to feed percentages directly into the center formula without showing the sample size or category counts. Imani returns the cards to the left rack. Expected proportions are useful because they generate expected counts for the actual sample size. The chi-square calculation compares count with count. She also stops another technician from opening the final decision panel. The current room cannot reject or fail to reject anything yet. Degrees of freedom and a significance criterion have not been supplied, so the correct scientific action is to keep the conclusion covered.
Imani uncovers the three compartments of the evidence tray for a moment. Inheritance route evidence answered how genomes were transmitted. Case evidence illustrated specific genotype-to-phenotype mechanisms. Statistical model evidence asks whether categorical observations are sufficiently consistent with a specified null model. The three kinds of reasoning can support one larger genetics investigation, yet none is interchangeable with another. That separation is why the final laboratory can remain memorable without reducing every genetics question to a single procedure.
The second optional Quick Recall appears only now. Imani points to the right gate and asks what kinds of data the Unit 5 chi-square goodness-of-fit procedure compares. ## 6. Observed–Expected Calculation Station — The Station Where Observed and Expected Were Kept in Separate Columns

*Observed counts come from the data, expected counts come from the null model, and each category contributes (O−E)²/E to the total chi-square statistic.*

The console rolls into the **Observed–Expected Calculation Station** and splits into three vertical sections. On your **left**, the observed column contains the categorical frequencies actually recorded in the study. Directly **ahead**, a glass calculator displays one category at a time beneath the expression (O−E)²/E. On your **right**, the expected column contains frequencies predicted by the null model from the total sample size and specified expected proportions. Imani locks the **three-compartment evidence tray** beside the station and leaves only the statistical-model compartment open. The physical separation is strict because observed and expected counts come from different sources of evidence.
Imani uncovers the left column and names **Observed count (O)**. These are the category frequencies actually recorded in the data. A counter above each bin is linked to the collected sample, not to the inheritance model. She then uncovers the right column and names **Expected count (E)**. These are the category frequencies predicted by the null model, often calculated from the total sample size and the expected proportions specified by that model. The two columns may contain similar numbers, but similarity does not make them the same kind of quantity.
The center calculator takes one observed value and its corresponding expected value. It subtracts E from O, squares the difference, and divides by E. Imani names this category-level quantity **Chi-square contribution**. Each category contributes (O−E)²/E to the total χ². Squaring removes the sign of the difference, so deviations above and below expectation do not cancel one another. Dividing by E scales the discrepancy relative to the expected size of that category. The machine preserves the contribution in a transparent compartment before moving to the next category.
Imani demonstrates the workflow with the arithmetic hidden behind the glass. You can see O enter from the left and E enter from the right. The calculator performs the operation and outputs one nonnegative contribution. The palace is teaching the structure of the calculation, while full arithmetic practice remains for Challenge Lab. The important memory is the source and role of each quantity. O is observed from data. E is expected from the null model. Each pair produces a category contribution, and the contributions are summed to form the overall χ² statistic.
A lab assistant tries to swap the column headers. The console rejects the change. An expected count cannot become observed merely because it looks plausible, and an observed count cannot be treated as the model prediction. Imani traces a line from the left column back to the collected data cards and a separate line from the right column back to the null-model rack in the previous room. Those two provenance lines remain visible while every calculation is performed.
The final category contribution drops into the center sum chamber. The console adds all the category contributions and displays one χ² statistic. Imani immediately covers the conclusion lights again. A large χ² indicates greater overall disagreement between observed and expected counts, but the number by itself does not yet supply the formal hypothesis decision. Its interpretation depends on the degrees of freedom and the chosen significance criterion. The next room contains those controls.
Before leaving, Imani asks you to redraw the station from memory. Observed count belongs on the left and comes from recorded data. Expected count belongs on the right and comes from the null model. The center transforms each matched pair through (O−E)²/E, then sums the contributions. If those three anchors are preserved, the formula no longer floats as an arbitrary sequence of symbols. It is a structured comparison of data against a model.
The far wall slides open to reveal the **Chi-Square Decision Console**. A degrees-of-freedom selector and critical-value table stand on the left, the calculated χ² enters the center, and two conclusion lamps wait on the right. Imani carries the summed statistic forward without changing any observed or expected count. The next room will decide what the statistic means under the chosen criterion.

## 7. Chi-Square Decision Console — The Console With No Accept-Null Button

*A chi-square decision requires degrees of freedom and a significance criterion, and the conclusion is stated as reject or fail to reject the null without treating a nonsignificant result as proof.*

The final door opens onto the **Chi-Square Decision Console**. On your **left**, a control bank displays the number of outcome categories, a degrees-of-freedom selector, a significance-level dial, and a critical-value table. Directly **ahead**, the χ² statistic from the previous station glows inside the central console. On your **right**, two conclusion lamps are mounted above separate doors. One reads REJECT THE NULL. The other reads FAIL TO REJECT THE NULL. There is no third door labeled ACCEPT THE NULL. Imani places the complete **three-compartment evidence tray** beneath the controls and uncovers every compartment. The laboratory can now connect inheritance mechanism, biological evidence, and statistical evidence without confusing the jobs of those evidence types.
Imani starts at the left selector and names **Degrees of freedom for AP goodness-of-fit**. For the AP goodness-of-fit setup used here, where expected proportions are fixed and no additional parameters are estimated from the data, degrees of freedom equal the number of outcome categories minus one. She keeps the task context printed above the selector. The formula is not offered as a universal rule for every chi-square procedure. It belongs to this fixed-proportion goodness-of-fit setup.
Next she turns the significance-level dial and selects an alpha criterion. The critical-value table responds to both alpha and degrees of freedom. Imani names the **Chi-square critical value**. A chi-square critical value depends on the chosen significance level and degrees of freedom and provides a threshold against which the calculated statistic can be evaluated. The table does not come from the observed counts themselves. It is part of the inferential decision framework supplied after the test setup is known.
A second display shows the corresponding probability interpretation, and Imani names **p-value and significance level**. A p-value describes how extreme a test statistic at least as large as the observed one would be under the null model. The chosen alpha, such as 0.05, is the significance threshold used for the decision. Imani removes a false sign claiming that alpha of 0.05 means there is a 95 percent probability the null hypothesis is true. The console cannot produce that statement because it is not what the p-value or alpha means.
The calculated χ² now enters the center decision track. One route compares the statistic with the relevant critical value. Another route compares the p-value with alpha. Both routes feed the same decision panel. Imani names the final relationship **Reject or fail to reject the null**. When the evidence exceeds the chosen threshold, the null hypothesis is rejected at that significance level. When it does not, the correct conclusion is to fail to reject the null hypothesis. The second outcome leaves the null model plausible under the available evidence. It does not prove the null model true.
A technician rolls in an ACCEPTED stamp. The right panel has no slot for it. Imani sends it back to storage. A nonsignificant result can occur when the observed data are reasonably compatible with the null model, when the sample is limited, or when the test lacks enough evidence to detect a departure. The workflow therefore protects the wording of the conclusion. Fail to reject is a statement about the available evidence under the chosen criterion. It is not a declaration that the null has been verified as fact.
The three-compartment evidence tray now becomes the final memory map for Unit 5. The inheritance-route compartment holds nuclear and organelle transmission distinctions, including qualified animal and plant parent-of-origin patterns. The biological-case compartment holds contextual disease examples and evidence-based variant language. The statistical-model compartment holds the null model, categorical observed and expected counts, χ² calculation, degrees of freedom, decision threshold, and final conclusion. Each compartment answers a different question, and the complete investigation becomes stronger because those questions remain distinguishable.
The third optional Quick Recall appears at the decision panel. Imani points to the two illuminated doors and asks for the correct hypothesis-decision phrases. Once you retrieve **Reject or fail to reject the null**, the console records the answer and closes the false accept-null slot permanently. She then asks you to replay the full statistical route. Define the null model, obtain expected categorical counts, compare them with observed counts, compute category contributions and χ², determine the relevant degrees of freedom and criterion, then state the evidence-based decision.
Imani closes the laboratory by sliding the three-compartment evidence tray into the gold generation ledger cabinet. The Unit 5 route can now be reconstructed from chromosomes and meiosis through inheritance models, probability, non-Mendelian mechanisms, linkage mapping, organelle inheritance, and statistical evidence. The final room does not turn chi-square into a verdict machine. It preserves a scientific habit. Start with a model, keep observations separate from expectations, calculate from defined quantities, apply the correct criterion, and state only the conclusion the evidence supports.
