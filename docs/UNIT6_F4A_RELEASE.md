## Narrative standard established

Journey 1 now serves as the Unit 6 F4 writing benchmark.

Every scene begins by placing the learner in a concrete left/center/right layout before the scientific state changes. Dr. Sora Han remains the recurring guide, while the actual biological structures keep their conventional identities. The same blue-and-gold DNA molecule, its original strand identities, its 5′/3′ arrowheads, and its copper origin tag remain traceable through the whole journey.

The story does not reset between loci. Each scene creates the problem solved by the next scene. Chromosome storage creates the need to separate plasmids; base classification leads to complementary pairing; antiparallel structure creates the directional replication problem; replication-model evidence selects semiconservative replication; the origin opens into moving forks; the fork exposes the need for primers; primer-dependent synthesis creates leading/lagging geometry; lagging fragments create the need for ligase and repair; completed linear DNA creates the chromosome-end maintenance problem.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

The visible story preserves the F1/F3 guards that matter most in this journey.

- DNA is the usual cellular hereditary material while RNA can serve as hereditary material in some biological systems.
- Prokaryotic circular and eukaryotic linear chromosome forms are presented as typical patterns, not universal rules.
- Plasmids remain extra-chromosomal DNA distinct from the main chromosome.
- Purine/pyrimidine classification remains distinct from complementary pairing.
- Sugar-phosphate backbone bonds remain visually separate from base-pair hydrogen bonds.
- Cellular RNA is described as typically single-stranded and capable of intramolecular base pairing.
- S phase is restricted to the eukaryotic cell-cycle context.
- Semiconservative, conservative, and dispersive models remain physically distinct and are resolved with Meselson–Stahl evidence.
- Origins, forks, helicase, and topoisomerase have different physical positions and jobs.
- Primase supplies the RNA primer; DNA polymerase extends from an available 3′ end.
- Both daughter strands obey 5′→3′ synthesis even though leading and lagging patterns differ.
- Ligase, helicase, and topoisomerase remain distinct enzymes with different jobs.
- Proofreading reduces replication errors without making replication error-free.
- Telomeres remain chromosome-end DNA/associated structures; telomerase remains an RNA-containing reverse-transcriptase enzyme.
