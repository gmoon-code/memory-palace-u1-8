## Narrative continuity

Journey 2 carries the same tagged gene through all eight scenes. Its template strand stays violet, the opposite DNA strand remains separate, and one red RNA transcript grows 5′→3′ before receiving only the processing steps appropriate to its cellular context.

The story keeps RNA roles distinct before transcription begins. mRNA remains a message, rRNA remains a structural/catalytic ribosome component, and tRNA remains the adaptor that carries specific amino-acid cargo while pairing an anticodon with an mRNA codon. The route then separates transcription from translation before selecting a template strand and promoter.

Once transcription starts, the learner can physically reconstruct the directional rule: RNA polymerase reads the DNA template 3′→5′ while RNA grows 5′→3′. The same transcript then reaches a fork. The prokaryotic route shows ribosome access without the eukaryotic cap/poly-A/splicing sequence, while the eukaryotic route continues through pre-mRNA processing, end modification, intron removal, exon joining, alternative splicing, and mature-mRNA export.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

The visible narrative preserves the F1/F3 distinctions that are especially vulnerable in this topic.

- RNA function depends on sequence and structure; “RNA” is not treated as one generic job.
- mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA retain separate roles.
- A tRNA anticodon is not called a codon.
- Gene expression can produce a functional RNA or a polypeptide product.
- Transcription and translation remain physically and mechanistically distinct.
- Eukaryotic nuclear transcription and cytoplasmic translation are distinguished from prokaryotic cellular organization.
- RNA polymerase uses one DNA template strand.
- A promoter remains a DNA region where transcription machinery assembles.
- A TATA box is presented only as one possible promoter element in some eukaryotic genes, not as a universal promoter definition.
- RNA synthesis remains 5′→3′ while the DNA template is read 3′→5′.
- Multiple RNA polymerases can increase transcript output without reversing synthesis direction.
- Many bacterial mRNAs can be translated without the eukaryotic cap/poly-A/splicing sequence, but the story does not claim that prokaryotic RNA never undergoes processing.
- The 5′ cap is distinguished from a translated codon.
- The polyadenylation signal is distinguished from the poly-A tail that is added later.
- Introns are removed and retained exons are joined; retained exon is not defined as universally protein-coding sequence.
- Alternative splicing changes the mature RNA product while the underlying gene DNA remains unchanged.
- Mature mRNA remains distinct from pre-mRNA.
