## Narrative continuity

Journey 3 begins with the mature mRNA produced in Journey 2 and keeps one red mRNA ribbon identifiable throughout the ordinary translation route. Its AUG start codon remains highlighted, its triplet spacing stays fixed, and the growing polypeptide changes only when the ribosome advances by one codon.

The learner first places translation in correct cellular geography. The route then locks the reading frame at AUG, interprets mRNA codons through the genetic code, charges tRNAs before delivery, organizes A/P/E traffic inside the ribosome, repeats codon recognition → peptide-bond formation → translocation during elongation, and terminates when a stop codon reaches the decoding position.

The final retroviral room remains physically separate from the ordinary translation line. A silver viral RNA genome is copied into DNA by reverse transcriptase and routed toward host-genome integration, preventing RNA→DNA information flow from being confused with translation.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

- Translation is placed on cytoplasmic ribosomes in prokaryotes and eukaryotes, including ribosomes associated with the cytoplasmic surface of rough ER in eukaryotes.
- Coupled transcription and translation in prokaryotes is explained through shared cellular geography and lack of a nucleus.
- Reading frame is kept distinct from an individual codon.
- AUG establishes translation initiation and specifies methionine in the initiation context.
- Translation stages remain initiation → elongation → termination.
- Codons are three-nucleotide mRNA units that specify amino acids or stop signals.
- Genetic-code redundancy is kept distinct from ambiguity.
- The genetic code is described as nearly universal, not absolutely universal.
- tRNA is charged before ribosomal codon recognition; the anticodon does not create or select the amino acid at the ribosome.
- Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase remains a real charging enzyme and is not substituted with a mnemonic character.
- A, P, and E are functional ribosomal tRNA-binding sites and are not ribosomal subunits.
- Bacterial 70S (30S + 50S) and eukaryotic cytosolic 80S (40S + 60S) values remain contextual enrichment and are not treated as additive mass units.
- Polypeptide elongation keeps codon recognition, peptide-bond formation, and translocation as distinct but connected events.
- Translocation advances the ribosome by one codon while preserving the reading frame.
- Stop codons do not encode amino acids and do not recruit a stop-codon tRNA.
- Termination machinery releases the polypeptide and allows translation-complex disassembly.
- Folding can begin during translation and continue after release; some proteins require chaperones or additional processing/modification.
- Retroviral reverse transcription remains RNA → DNA; viral DNA can integrate into the host genome and later be transcribed and translated.
