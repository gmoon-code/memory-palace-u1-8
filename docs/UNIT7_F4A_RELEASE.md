## Narrative benchmark established

Journey 1 now establishes the Unit 7 F4 writing benchmark.

The observatory is one clockwise ten-location route under a glass dome. Every scene establishes left, center, and right geography before the population or evidence state changes. Dr. Imani Vale remains the recurring guide, and the same transparent population ledger remains the continuity object. The ledger keeps phenotype or genotype category, viable reproductive offspring contributed to the next generation, and population frequency visible without replacing the actual organisms or evidence.

The story uses one continuing calibration problem. The observatory is falsely labeling every visible change as evolution. Each room repairs one diagnostic relationship, so vocabulary grows from visible biological action rather than appearing as an isolated list.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

The visible story preserves the F1 and F3 safeguards central to Journey 1.

- Darwin and Galápagos material remain historical/evidentiary context and do not replace the mechanism of natural selection.
- Biogeography remains geographic evidence for descent, dispersal, and diversification.
- Descent with modification remains a historical population pattern across generations.
- Population evolution is explicitly separated from within-lifetime individual change.
- Phenotypic variation is present before selection operates, and visible difference is not automatically treated as heritable.
- Adaptation remains a heritable trait whose reproductive effect depends on a particular environment.
- Competition is connected to limited resources, while population change is driven through differential reproductive contribution.
- Evolutionary fitness remains reproductive success relative to others in the population, not strength or survival alone.
- Fitness effects remain environment dependent.
- Within-lifetime body-condition change is separated from heritable generational change.
- Selection is explicitly nonrandom while mutation originates randomly with respect to fitness.
- The sickle-cell case preserves heterozygote advantage in malaria-endemic environments and never treats sickle-cell disease itself as the protective state.
- Directional, stabilizing, and disruptive selection remain physically and causally distinct.
- Sexual selection includes mate choice and competition and is evaluated through reproductive success even when a trait carries survival costs.
- Artificial selection is produced by human-directed reproductive choice and remains distinct from natural selection.

## Memorability and spatial design

The main selection sequence repeatedly uses a simulated medium-ground-finch population so the learner does not have to relearn a new organism at every locus. Different biological examples appear only when the locked science requires them, such as the sickle-cell heterozygote case and sexual-selection arena.

The story maintains one stable sequence of causal questions.

1. What does the evidence support?
2. Where is evolutionary change measured?
3. What variation is available before selection?
4. How do limited resources produce unequal reproduction?
5. What does fitness measure?
6. How does a changing environment alter outcomes without making an individual evolve?
7. How can molecular variation connect to environment-dependent fitness?
8. What do different relative-fitness patterns do to a population distribution?
9. How can access to mates change reproductive success?
10. What changes when humans choose which individuals reproduce?
