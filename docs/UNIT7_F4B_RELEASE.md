## Continuous story design

Journey 2 keeps two transparent island gene-pool tanks as the central continuity system. The focal Hardy-Weinberg locus uses blue A and amber a allele copies that remain individually countable as the populations move through demographic events. Population size and generation are visible separately from allele frequency.

A second marked locus is used for the mutation demonstration. This prevents the mutation scene from silently turning the later two-allele Hardy-Weinberg model into a three-allele system.

The route follows one escalating audit problem. The island network is producing incorrect explanations for changing allele frequencies. It confuses chance with selection, demographic sampling with migration, p/q notation with dominance, and expected Hardy-Weinberg values with directly observed genotype frequencies. Dr. Imani Vale and the learner cannot clear the audit until every allele movement, sampling event, and model quantity has a reconstructable origin.

## Scientific safeguards carried into visible prose

- A population boundary is established before gene-pool or allele-frequency calculations.
- A gene pool remains the alleles of reproducing population members, not the population itself.
- Allele-frequency change across generations establishes microevolution without automatically identifying the mechanism.
- Mutation creates new alleles and is random with respect to fitness.
- Mutation and natural selection remain causally distinct.
- Generation time is distinguished from mutation probability per replication.
- Random-process sampling is separated from nonrandom natural selection.
- Genetic drift remains chance allele-frequency change, strongest in small populations and capable of reducing variation without being adaptive.
- Bottleneck effect remains genetic drift after a severe reduction when survivors are a chance, nonrepresentative sample at the focal locus.
- Founder effect remains a small founding sample establishing a new population while the source population does not have to collapse.
- Gene flow requires actual movement of fertile individuals or gametes across a population boundary and tends to counter divergence.
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium remains an idealized, locus-specific null model.
- Large population, no migration, no mutation, random mating, and no natural selection remain five separate model conditions.
- Nonrandom mating by itself can alter genotype frequencies while allele frequencies remain unchanged.
- p and q remain arbitrary allele-frequency labels with no inherent dominance or recessiveness.
- p², 2pq, and q² remain expected genotype frequencies under Hardy-Weinberg conditions.
- Observed genotype counts remain separate from expected genotype frequencies.
- Allele frequencies from genotype counts are obtained by direct allele-copy counting across 2N.
- Square-root inference from a recessive phenotype is permitted only when the phenotype reliably identifies the recessive homozygote and Hardy-Weinberg assumptions are justified.

## Narrative and spatial benchmark

Every scene establishes left, center, and right geography before population counts, allele movements, or model states change. Island A, Island B, the color conventions, the population-size marker, the generation label, and the transparent population ledger remain stable.

The first eight scenes make mechanisms physically diagnostic. Mutation produces a new allele at a separate locus. Drift uses blind finite sampling. Bottleneck uses a severe demographic reduction. Founder effect uses a small colonizing group while the source remains large. Gene flow visibly crosses a population boundary.

The final six scenes make Hardy-Weinberg quantities physically diagnostic. Observed allele counts enter from one side, assumptions remain controlled, p/q notation is separated from genotype categories, expected p²/2pq/q² values remain model outputs, and direct observed genotype counts are recounted independently before the final comparison.
