## Continuous story design

Journey 4 uses one six-taxon navigation dossier with fixed taxon identities and a persistent node map.

The sampled taxa are Lancelet, Shark, Salamander, Mouse, Lizard, and Pigeon.

The internal node IDs N0 through N4 remain attached to the same inferred common-ancestor positions. Branches can rotate and candidate hypotheses can be revised, but the student always has stable identities to reconstruct the relationship.

The central navigation failure is that the archive confuses the appearance of a drawing with the ancestry represented by the drawing. It reads long branches as more evolved, nearby labels as sister taxa, early-branching lineages as primitive, extant tips as ancestors, and a rotated tree as a different history.

## Scientific safeguards carried into visible prose

- Taxonomy, systematics, phylogenetics, and phylogeny remain distinct scientific roles.
- A phylogeny remains a hypothesis about evolutionary history and relationships.
- Trees and cladograms remain testable and revisable hypotheses.
- Cladogram branch lengths carry no implied scale unless a scale is explicitly encoded.
- Some phylogenetic trees may encode time or amount of change when calibrated.
- Branch rotation around a node leaves the ancestry relationship unchanged.
- Root and internal nodes represent inferred ancestral lineages.
- Extant tips remain descendants and never become ancestors of other extant taxa.
- A lineage remains a line of descent through time.
- A clade includes a common ancestor and all descendants.
- Sister taxa are the two lineages descending immediately from the same common-ancestor node.
- Early-branching or basal wording is tied only to branching position and never to primitive or less evolved status.
- Ancestral and derived characters are interpreted relative to the focal comparison.
- Shared derived characters are mapped onto a branch inherited from a common ancestor.
- A synapomorphy remains a shared derived character inherited from the relevant most recent common ancestor.
- An outgroup stays outside the focal ingroup and is never made into the ingroup ancestor.
- Outgroup comparison helps infer ancestral-versus-derived polarity.
- Morphological, fossil, DNA, and protein evidence can all contribute to phylogenetic construction.
- Molecular evidence can support or revise relationships without making phylogenies unrevisable facts.
- Monophyletic, paraphyletic, and polyphyletic groups remain defined by common-ancestor and descendant membership.
- Parsimony compares candidate hypotheses by required character-state changes and is never presented as infallible or the only phylogenetic method.
