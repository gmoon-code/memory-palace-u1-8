## Scientific safeguards

- Species boundaries remain tied to reproductive isolation and sufficiently reduced or prevented gene flow.
- The biological species concept remains limited to contexts where sexual reproduction makes interbreeding and viable-fertile offspring meaningful.
- Allopatric speciation keeps geographic separation visible throughout the pathway.
- Sympatric speciation keeps geographic overlap visible while gene flow is reduced by biological mechanisms within the shared area.
- Geography and reproductive isolation remain separate variables.
- Prezygotic barriers act before zygote formation.
- Habitat and temporal isolation reduce mating by encounter opportunity for different reasons.
- Behavioral, mechanical, and gametic isolation occupy different positions in the reproductive sequence.
- Postzygotic barriers begin only after fertilization.
- Reduced hybrid viability, reduced hybrid fertility, and hybrid breakdown remain separated by timing and outcome.
- Hybrid sterility is never reduced to chromosome-number mismatch as a universal cause.
- Punctuated equilibrium, gradualism, and stasis describe evolutionary tempo and are not speciation mechanisms.
- Divergence can contribute to speciation only when reproductive isolation develops.
- Adaptive radiation, convergent evolution, macroevolution, and extinction remain distinct long-timescale patterns.
