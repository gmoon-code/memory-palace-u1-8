## Journey routes

### U8-J1 · Behavioral Response Institute

**Premise.** The Behavioral Response Institute begins with observable cues and responses, then separates immediate mechanism, evolutionary explanation, inherited predisposition, learning, movement, communication, social behavior, and plant responses so behavior never becomes a loose list of labels.

**Mission.** Carry one stimulus-response tablet through twelve rooms. At each room, identify the cue, biological responder, response pattern, and consequence before naming the behavior or learning mechanism.

**Continuity object.** one transparent stimulus-response tablet with fixed fields for cue, responder, immediate mechanism, experience, behavioral output, and fitness/homeostasis consequence

**Route**

- **U8-L01 · Behavior and environmental response** — Institute entry response bay; the cue-response workbench at the entrance
- **U8-L02 · Proximate and ultimate questions** — Two-Level Causation Desk; the split analysis desk immediately beyond the entry bay
- **U8-L03 · Innate and learned behavior** — Behavior Origin Comparison Bay; the paired baseline-and-experience panels
- **U8-L04 · Fixed action pattern and sign stimulus** — Trigger Sequence Chamber; the red-cue trigger apparatus
- **U8-L05 · Migration and environmental orientation** — Migration Navigation Dome; the long-distance route globe
- **U8-L06 · Kinesis and taxis** — Movement Gradient Arena; the floor arena with one stable stimulus gradient
- **U8-L07 · Signals and communication** — Communication Sender-Receiver Gallery; the central signal-channel bridge
- **U8-L08 · Imprinting and sensitive periods** — Developmental Learning Timeline Room; the highlighted sensitive-period window
- **U8-L09 · Spatial, associative, and social learning** — Learning Modes Wing; the three-panel learning comparison wall
- **U8-L10 · Foraging and mating strategies** — Fitness Tradeoff Hall; the energy-risk and mating allocation board
- **U8-L11 · Cooperation, altruism, and inclusive fitness** — Inclusive Fitness Ledger Chamber; the direct-and-indirect fitness accounting table
- **U8-L12 · Plant environmental responses and defenses** — Plant Response Greenhouse; the cue-to-growth/defense bench

### U8-J2 · Ecosystem Energy Exchange Hall

**Premise.** The Ecosystem Energy Exchange Hall follows energy from organism budgets into ecosystem structure, producer capture, feeding relationships, production, and trophic transfer while matter conservation stays visibly separate.

**Mission.** Carry one two-column energy-and-matter ledger from organism intake through trophic transfer. Every transfer must show where energy enters, where it becomes less available, and where matter remains conserved.

**Continuity object.** one transparent energy-matter ledger with an energy column that can dissipate as heat and a matter column that remains traceable through organisms and reservoirs

**Route**

- **U8-L13 · Organism energy budget** — Organism Energy Budget Counter; the intake-expenditure balance board
- **U8-L14 · Endothermy, ectothermy, and metabolic rate** — Thermal Regulation Bay; the heat-source comparison platform
- **U8-L15 · Ecosystems, biotic factors, and abiotic factors** — Ecological Scale Atrium; the nested ecosystem-scale model
- **U8-L16 · Energy laws and matter conservation** — Energy-Matter Split Gate; the paired energy-flow and matter-cycle diagram
- **U8-L17 · Autotrophs, heterotrophs, and chemosynthesis** — Carbon and Energy Acquisition Workshop; the carbon-source/energy-source matrix
- **U8-L18 · Trophic levels and decomposers** — Trophic Ladder Tower; the feeding-position ladder beside the detritus chute
- **U8-L19 · Food chains and food webs** — Food-Web Projection Room; the chain-to-web arrow wall
- **U8-L20 · Primary and secondary production** — Production Accounting Ledger; the GPP-respiration-NPP accounting board
- **U8-L21 · Trophic transfer and ecosystem energy propagation** — Trophic Transfer Pyramid Exit; the variable-efficiency energy pyramid

### U8-J3 · Biogeochemical Cycle Watershed

**Premise.** The Biogeochemical Cycle Watershed uses the same reservoir-transfer frame for water, carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus while keeping the chemistry and dominant reservoirs of each cycle distinct.

**Mission.** Move labeled matter through abiotic and biotic reservoirs using only valid transfer processes. Each cycle must return to the shared reservoir-transfer logic without borrowing a process from another cycle.

**Continuity object.** one watershed reservoir board with fixed pool boxes and transfer-arrow slots; the tracked substance changes by cycle, but reservoir versus process never changes meaning

**Route**

- **U8-L22 · Biogeochemical cycles and reservoirs** — Watershed Reservoir Map Hub; the pool-versus-process map key
- **U8-L23 · Water cycle** — Water Cycle Watershed; the atmosphere-surface-groundwater cross-section
- **U8-L24 · Carbon cycle** — Carbon Cycle Reservoir Terrace; the atmosphere-organic-carbon exchange wall
- **U8-L25 · Nitrogen cycle** — Nitrogen Transformation Gallery; the chemically directional nitrogen pathway
- **U8-L26 · Phosphorus cycle** — Phosphorus Sedimentary Basin; the rock-soil-water-food-web cross-section

### U8-J4 · Population Survey and Demography Reserve

**Premise.** The Population Survey and Demography Reserve follows one population from field sampling through spatial pattern, age-specific survival, population-change accounting, and life-history allocation.

**Mission.** Keep one marked population identity while changing how it is measured. Density, dispersion, demography, survivorship, births/deaths/migration, and life-history traits must remain different measurements of the same population scale.

**Continuity object.** one marked field population with a persistent census ID, mapped boundary, cohort ledger, and population-size counter N

**Route**

- **U8-L27 · Population ecology, density, and sampling** — Reserve Population Survey Grid; the marked quadrat and population boundary
- **U8-L28 · Population dispersion patterns** — Dispersion Pattern Field; the three equal-density plots
- **U8-L29 · Demography and life tables** — Demography Census Station; the age-class life-table desk
- **U8-L30 · Survivorship curves** — Survivorship Curve Observatory; the common age-versus-survival graph
- **U8-L31 · Population change accounting** — Population Change Ledger; the dN/dt births-deaths board with migration side lane

**Mission.** Keep the same N, rmax, time axis, and resource condition visible while moving from exponential to logistic models and then testing regulatory factors.

**Continuity object.** one population-growth dashboard with persistent N, time, rmax, K/resource readout, per-capita rate panel, and graph axes

### U8-J6 · Community Interaction District

**Premise.** The Community Interaction District places several species in one shared landscape so habitat, niche, competition, predation, symbiosis, disturbance, and succession can be interpreted from effects on real populations.

**Mission.** Carry one community map with persistent species identities and resource layers. Every interaction changes a sign, resource access, abundance, or occupied niche, and those changes must remain visible into the next block.

**Continuity object.** one shared community map with persistent species A/B/C identities, habitat layers, resource layers, abundance markers, and interaction-sign badges

**Route**

- **U8-L42 · Competition, exclusion, and resource partitioning** — Competition Resource Axis; the shared limiting-resource track

### U8-J8 · Environmental Change Response Center

**Premise.** The Environmental Change Response Center traces distinct causes of ecological change from genetic variation and biological invasion through habitat alteration, harvesting, pollution, eutrophication, ocean acidification, and large-scale geographic drivers.

**Mission.** Use one baseline landscape-and-community record. Each room changes one driver, then traces the mechanism to population or community consequences without substituting a generic “human impact” explanation.

**Continuity object.** one baseline landscape record with habitat area, focal population size, community composition, trophic structure, water chemistry, and geographic context fields

**Route**
