## Narrative architecture carried into prose

Journey 2 follows one continuous ecological accounting route through nine fixed locations. Every scene establishes the exact room and the left, center, and right scientific anchors before the ecological state changes. Dr. Mira Sen remains the recurring Unit 8 guide. One transparent energy-matter ledger persists through the entire route with a visible ENERGY column and MATTER column.

The plot is built around a specific accounting failure. The Ecosystem Energy Exchange Hall has begun treating energy and matter as though they follow the same route. Each station becomes intelligible only after the learner can identify where energy enters, where it is transformed, where it becomes less available for biological work, where biomass is produced or transferred, and where matter remains conserved and traceable.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

- Organism energy budgets keep acquisition, expenditure, maintenance, growth, reproduction, storage, and sustained deficit distinct.
- Endotherms and ectotherms are distinguished by predominant heat source. Neither group is defined by a simplistic constant-temperature versus variable-temperature rule.
- Absolute metabolic rate generally rises with body size while mass-specific metabolic rate generally falls.
- Population, community, ecosystem, and biome remain distinct ecological levels.
- Biotic and abiotic factors remain separated by living or biological versus nonliving physical or chemical status.
- The first law, second law, and conservation of matter remain scientifically distinct.
- Energy is transferred and transformed but never created. Energy ultimately leaves ecosystems largely as heat and is not recycled through trophic relationships.
- Matter remains conserved through ordinary ecological transformations and can cycle among reservoirs.
- Autotrophs build organic molecules from inorganic carbon using external energy sources. Chemosynthetic organisms capture and transform chemical energy from inorganic substances and do not create energy.
- Heterotrophs obtain organic carbon and energy from organisms or organic matter.
- Trophic positions remain based on the source of energy and matter. Decomposers return inorganic nutrients without recycling energy.
- Food-chain and food-web arrows point from resource or prey toward the consumer receiving energy and matter.
- GPP, producer respiration, NPP, the relationship `NPP = GPP − R`, and secondary production remain separate.
- Primary production may be photosynthetic or chemosynthetic.
- About ten percent remains a useful average trophic-transfer rule of thumb and never becomes a fixed law.
- Changes in incoming energy or producer biomass can propagate through population size, trophic structure, and ecosystem dynamics.
