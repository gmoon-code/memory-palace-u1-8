## Narrative architecture carried into prose

Journey 5 keeps one population-growth dashboard visible through five fixed control rooms. The same N counter, time axis, rmax dial, K and resource readout, per-capita panel, and graph axes persist while only one model assumption or regulation test changes. Every scene establishes left, center, and right before the scientific state changes. Dr. Mira Sen remains the guide and does not replace equations, graphs, populations, or environmental factors with mnemonic characters.

The route is designed to make the major population-growth confusions spatially difficult to collapse. Exponential growth is tied to its idealized assumptions and J-shaped graph. Carrying capacity occupies its own environment-controlled ceiling and can move when conditions change. Logistic growth then adds the K-dependent fraction and shows why growth slows as N approaches K. The final two test bays keep low- and high-density populations in matched positions so classification depends on per-capita effects rather than total losses.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

- `dN/dt` remains population change per unit time and is not labeled as the per-capita growth rate.
- `rmax` remains the maximum per-capita population growth parameter used in the AP exponential and logistic models.
- Exponential growth does not add a constant number per unit time. With a constant positive per-capita rate, absolute growth increases as N rises.
- Exponential growth remains an idealized model for intervals with abundant resources and weak limiting factors.
- Carrying capacity K remains environment-dependent and is not treated as a permanent species constant.
- N, K, and rmax remain separate variables or parameters with different meanings.
- The logistic model keeps `dN/dt = rmaxN((K − N)/K)` tied to the shrinking `(K − N)/K` term as N approaches K.
- Real populations are not required to follow a perfect S-shaped curve or remain exactly at K.
- Density-dependent regulation is diagnosed when a per-capita birth or death effect changes systematically with density.
- Density-independent effects are diagnosed when per-capita impact does not depend systematically on population density.
- Total losses are not used by themselves to classify density dependence.
- Quantitative calculation remains gated to the Challenge Lab. The permanent narrative teaches model structure, variable meaning, assumptions, and graph interpretation.
