## Narrative architecture carried into prose

Journey 7 carries one fixed community sample card through four observatory locations. Species counts, total N, the diversity readout, and a disturbance or species-removal intervention panel remain attached to the same community evidence while the question changes. The learner begins by separating number of species from proportional composition, then translates those counts into the AP Simpson equation, widens the frame from species diversity to biodiversity, and finally tests how particular species influence community structure.

The route preserves the locked F3 left-center-right geometry at every locus. Richness remains on the count side, relative abundance remains in the proportional-distribution workspace, and species-diversity interpretation remains separate until both measurements have been compared. The Simpson table keeps species-specific lowercase n physically separate from total community-sample capital N. The resilience dome keeps genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity on distinct panels before a disturbance is introduced. The removal test keeps abundance and habitat formation visible while a species is reduced so keystone and foundation effects can be distinguished from evidence.

## Scientific safeguards carried into prose

- Species richness remains the number of species represented in the community or sample.
- Relative abundance remains the proportion of individuals represented by each species.
- Species diversity incorporates both richness and relative abundance and does not become a synonym for broader biodiversity.
- The AP Simpson diversity index remains `1 − Σ(n/N)²`.
- Lowercase n remains the number of individuals of a particular species. Capital N remains the total number of organisms of all species in the sample.
- Larger values in this AP form remain associated with greater diversity because dominance by a few species lowers the index.
- Calculation mechanics are visible in the narrative, while arithmetic practice remains reserved for the application layer.
- Biodiversity remains explicitly separated into genetic, species, and ecosystem levels.
- Species diversity remains one component of biodiversity.
- Greater diversity is described as able to support resilience and some ecosystem functions in many contexts without becoming a universal guarantee.
- Keystone species remain defined by disproportionately large effects on community structure relative to abundance.
- Foundation species remain distinct through high abundance or habitat-forming effects.
- Keystone status remains dependent on the ecological community and interaction network. No familiar taxon is treated as universally keystone.
