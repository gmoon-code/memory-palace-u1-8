# AP Chemistry F0C science component coverage plan

## Status

F0C is complete with an inventoried CED/public College Board laboratory source basis and all seven F0B source gaps preserved. Every one of the 91 CED topics now has an explicit component classification for concepts, Memory Objects, equations, calculations, graphs, diagrams, data tables, particulate models, laboratory contexts, and vectors.

This is a planning artifact. It creates no AP Chemistry runtime curriculum.

## Component-state rules

**Required** means later AP Chemistry content must include the component somewhere in the topic's instructional coverage. **Supporting** means the component may be used when it improves explanation or AP-style reasoning at the teacher-PPT depth. **Not primary** means the topic does not require that representation as a main instructional component. **Excluded** records a source-supported boundary that later content must not cross.

Every topic requires a concept representation and a Memory Object pathway. AP Chemistry uses equations, calculations, graphs, diagrams, data tables, particulate models, and laboratory contexts as declared course capabilities. Vector representation is not used in the current AP Chemistry package contract.

## Source basis

The CED controls required content, topic order, suggested science practices, and exclusions. The teacher PowerPoints control intended student depth. Zumdahl 11e controls chemistry accuracy within CED scope. Scoring guides provide assessment-demand evidence. Teacher-specific laboratory materials are not being used. By explicit user directive, the CED/public College Board laboratory guidance is the lab-source basis. F0C does not invent or attribute College Board procedures and does not claim that its topic-level lab candidates are official College Board lab assignments.

The CED requires a minimum of 25 percent of instructional time in laboratory investigations, at least 16 hands-on investigations, and at least 6 guided-inquiry investigations. F0C therefore preserves `lab_context` as a course capability and maps all 91 topics to project lab-planning classes using the existing experimental-reasoning priority. The exact 16-lab slate remains a later Challenge Lab design task.

## Critical scope guards

### Topic 8.11  pH and Solubility

The CED requires a qualitative relationship between pH and salt solubility and explicitly excludes calculations of solubility as a function of pH. F0C therefore marks those calculations **excluded**.

### Topic 9.10  Cell Potential Under Nonstandard Conditions

The teacher PPT marks the Nernst equation as not required. The CED frames Topic 9.10 qualitatively through the relationship among cell potential, Q, K, E°, and distance from equilibrium without supplying a required Nernst-equation calculation. F0C therefore does not require Nernst calculations.

### Topic 7.8  Representations of Equilibrium

The CED explicitly requires a particulate model. That requirement is preserved even though the teacher PPT coverage is partial. Later content may teach the required particulate representation while keeping depth bounded to the CED.

### Topic 9.7  Coupled Reactions

The CED requires coupled-reaction reasoning, while F0B located no direct teacher-PPT treatment. F0C requires symbolic reaction representation and supporting quantitative reasoning only at the CED and Zumdahl-supported level. The teacher-depth gap remains unresolved.
