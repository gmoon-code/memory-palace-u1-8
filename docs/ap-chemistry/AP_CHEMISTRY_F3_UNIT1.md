## Mnemonic design boundary

F3 locks a concrete object or mechanism for every Memory Object. Examples include the unit-canceling tile track for dimensional analysis, isotope twin badges for isotopes, a binding-energy peak rail for PES peak position, a four-control attraction console for periodic-trend causes, and a neutral-formula balance for ionic compound charge balance.

These objects establish what must be visible and what interaction must encode the chemistry. F3 does not yet write scene prose, character dialogue, guides, transitions, or finales. The later narrative phase can improve surface wording and dramatic action while preserving the locked scientific interaction.

## Retrieval modes

Retrieval is not limited to vocabulary recognition. Quantitative records require reconstruction or use of the relevant equation/procedure. Graph and data-table records require interpretation. Diagram and particulate-model records require translation between representations. Scope guards require discrimination of what AP Chemistry does and does not assess.

Eight explicit confusable sets are locked for later discrimination work, including mole versus molar mass versus Avogadro constant, shell versus subshell, PES peak position versus relative intensity, and ionization energy versus electron affinity versus electronegativity.

Exact spelling is used selectively for high-value scientific vocabulary. It is not imposed on every relationship or calculation record.

## Source boundary

The supplied academic sources do not provide pronunciation records. F3 therefore leaves pronunciation fields explicitly deferred rather than inventing authoritative pronunciations.

Application questions are also deferred. Every Memory Object includes an application prompt specification tied to the locked student-depth note, but the actual student-facing question and answer key remain for the later question-authoring phase.
