# Z3 Narrative Draft

## 0. The Alarm Beneath the Fountain

At 12:14 in the morning, the Sky Hotel tries to drown its own lobby.

The first warning is not the alarm.

It is the fountain.

One moment the black-marble basin in the center of the lobby is sending a quiet column of water toward the glass ceiling. The next, thunder cracks somewhere above the tower and every light goes out.

For half a second, the hotel disappears.

Then emergency power catches.

The fountain explodes upward.

Water strikes the bronze chandelier three stories above you and comes down in a silver sheet. Guests would normally be crossing the circular lobby from the south entrance toward the elevators. At this hour there are only empty chairs, abandoned luggage carts, and you standing ankle-deep in water that was definitely not there ten seconds ago.

Across the west wall, the hotel's living garden gives a shudder.

Its irrigation lights blink out.

Broad leaves that had been spread toward the atrium begin to fold downward.

"Move."

Mara Vale comes through the east service door at a run.

She is wearing a dark raincoat over hotel-engineering coveralls, though the raincoat has already failed at its one obvious responsibility. Water drips from the shoulders. A brass tool belt knocks against her hip as she crosses the flooded floor.

She does not waste time staring at the fountain.

She goes straight to the control pedestal on its east side, flips open a brass panel, and plants both hands on the console.

Above her, the hotel rises around a hollow central shaft of glass.

From the lobby you can look almost all the way to the roof. Balconies ring the atrium at different heights. Greenhouse levels glow faintly above. Higher still, the spa windows are dark. Near the top of the tower, the aquarium's blue emergency lamps have come on.

One illuminated water line runs through the entire structure.

It begins beneath the fountain.

From there it branches into the west living wall, climbs through the greenhouse levels, passes the thermal system and aquarium, then reaches the rooftop reservoir.

Every branch is changing color.

Greenhouse reserve.

Aquarium emergency circulation.

Living wall pressure loss.

Roof reservoir chemical alarm.

Mara reads the screen once.

"Main controller took the lightning strike."

You step beside her.

A red-blue trace jerks across the lower corner of the display.

"What's that?"

"Reservoir pH."

The line swings hard toward red, recoils, then shoots blue.

Mara's mouth tightens.

"Bad sensor data. Controller went offline, so half the chemistry channels are probably reporting nonsense."

She says it with the calm certainty of someone who has repaired this hotel at three in the morning before and resents being asked to do it again.

You watch the trace move.

Red.

Blue.

Red again.

Then you notice the event log beneath it.

Four lines.

**00:14:08  RESERVOIR pH DEVIATION**

**00:14:10  PRESSURE SURGE DETECTED**

**00:14:12  CENTRAL CONTROL BUS LOST**

**00:14:13  EMERGENCY WATER ROUTING ACTIVE**

You read them twice.

"Mara."

"If you're about to tell me the lobby is flooding, I have formed a preliminary opinion."

"The pH alarm came first."

Her hand stops over the reset switch.

You point.

"Four seconds before the controller died."

Mara leans closer.

The fountain crashes behind you. Water runs around the base of the pedestal and toward the west wall.

She scrolls backward through the log.

Same order.

Chemical deviation.

Pressure surge.

Controller failure.

Mara's expression changes by almost nothing, which is enough to make you uneasy.

"That's inconvenient," she says.

"Because the dead controller didn't cause it."

"Because I had a much shorter evening planned."

Another warning sounds.

The rooftop greenhouse icon turns amber.

Then the aquarium.

The problem is no longer confined to a number on a screen.

The living wall beside you is visibly wilting. Pumps somewhere high above the atrium shut down one after another with dull mechanical thumps. The roof reservoir feeds water to systems that keep plants, animals, and environmental controls running throughout the hotel. If that water supply becomes chemically unstable, the shutdown will spread.

Living systems depend on water, and here the dependency is drawn through the building in one glowing blue line.

Mara pulls a palm-sized object from her belt.

It looks like a thick piece of clear glass framed in brass.

"Scanner?" you ask.

"Molecular diagnostic scanner."

"Of course you carry one."

"You carry a phone. Mine is less disappointing."

She taps the control pedestal again.

The elevator icons remain gray.

**FLOOD PROTECTION LOCK**

"Central lifts are sealed," she says. "Water reached the lower shaft sensors."

You look upward.

The tower is built around the same water system that is failing, and the normal way to the roof runs through the middle of it.

"Stairs?"

"Service stairs don't connect continuously. The hotel was designed by people who thought emergencies should have themes."

A sharp metallic bang comes from the north side of the lobby.

Both of you turn.

Behind the fountain, a pipe coupling has torn loose from the wall.

A jet of water shoots into a transparent chute that rises beside the north wall. The chute had looked decorative when you entered the hotel, a vertical ribbon of glass climbing toward the mezzanine.

Now it fills in seconds.

Water surges upward through it.

Mara looks at the blue route map.

The north chute connects to the first maintenance level.

Above that, the route continues through the hotel systems one floor at a time until it reaches the roof.

"That's our way up," she says.

You look at the fountain, then at the living wall, then at the pH trace still whipping between red and blue.

"And what exactly are we looking for?"

Mara starts across the lobby.

"Evidence."

"Specific."

"I am being specific."

She reaches the north wall and stops beneath the rising chute.

Water is behaving differently in every part of the hotel already. It clings to the glass. It gathers into sheets. It carries heat through the building, freezes in upper systems, dissolves chemicals in the reservoir, and participates in the acid-base chemistry that the roof sensor is screaming about.

Those behaviors are not separate tricks. They come from how water molecules are built and how they interact with one another and with other substances. If the water itself has somehow changed, the evidence should appear again and again as you climb.

If it has not, then every normal behavior will eliminate another explanation.

Mara raises the glass scanner toward the chute.

"We start with the water."

A second pressure surge hits.

The transparent chute groans.

A seam above the mezzanine bursts open, and water pours across the north wall in a roaring silver sheet.

The emergency stair beside it unlocks with a heavy click.

You and Mara climb.

Below, the Life Fountain continues to overflow.

Above, the pH trace flashes red, then blue, then red again.

And somewhere between the lobby and the roof, something is turning one ordinary stream of water into a building-wide emergency.
