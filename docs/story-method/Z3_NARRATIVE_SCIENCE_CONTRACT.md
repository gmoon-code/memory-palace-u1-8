## Locked scientific payload

### Scene 0 · Life Fountain

**MO-APBIO-U1-W001 · Properties of water overview**  
Water’s polarity and hydrogen bonding contribute to cohesion, adhesion, capillary action, temperature moderation, evaporative cooling, the unusual density of ice, solvent behavior, and pH-related chemistry. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W002 · Living systems depend on water**  
Living systems depend on the properties of water to sustain life. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
The opening must establish a genuine systems-level reason to care about water. The later properties should feel like consequences of molecular structure and interaction, not an announced vocabulary list.

### Scene 1 · Polar Waterfall

**MO-APBIO-U1-W003 · Water polarity**  
Water is polar because oxygen and hydrogen share electrons unequally in polar covalent O–H bonds. Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W004 · Partial charges**  
Unequal electron sharing gives oxygen a partial negative charge and the hydrogens partial positive charges. These are partial charges, not full ionic charges. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W005 · Water polarity enables hydrogen bonding**  
Water polarity contributes to hydrogen bonding between water molecules and within or between biological molecules. The hydrogen-bond interaction between neighboring water molecules must remain distinct from the covalent O–H bonds inside each water molecule. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
The student should be able to picture the bent water molecule, the unequal electron distribution, δ− near oxygen, δ+ near hydrogen, and the resulting attraction between neighboring molecules as one causal sequence.

### Scene 2 · Cohesion Pool

**MO-APBIO-U1-W006 · Cohesion**  
Cohesion is attraction among molecules of the same kind. Hydrogen bonding contributes strongly to cohesion among water molecules. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W007 · Surface tension**  
Surface tension results from cohesive interactions among water molecules at the surface. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W008 · Adhesion**  
Adhesion is attraction of water to other polar or charged substances. Exact term required.

Narrative obligation  
Cohesion, surface tension, and adhesion must be distinguishable through what the water physically does. The story cannot collapse cohesion and adhesion into generic “stickiness.”

### Scene 3 · Xylem Tower

**MO-APBIO-U1-W009 · Cohesion and plant transport**  
Water cohesion contributes to the upward transport of water in plants. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W010 · Adhesion in xylem**  
Adhesion contributes to water interacting with xylem and cell walls and helps oppose downward movement due to gravity. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W011 · Capillary action**  
Cohesion among water molecules and adhesion to hydrophilic surfaces together contribute to capillary movement in narrow spaces and to water transport in plants. Exact term required.

Narrative obligation  
Capillary action must arise from the combined effects of cohesion and adhesion. The scene must avoid presenting it as a single force or as a complete explanation for all long-distance water transport in plants.

### Scene 4 · Thermal Bath

**MO-APBIO-U1-W012 · High specific heat capacity**  
Water resists rapid temperature change because it has a high specific heat capacity. This property contributes to temperature stability in organisms and environments. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W013 · Hydrogen-bond mechanism for specific heat**  
Hydrogen bonding contributes to water’s high specific heat because energy is absorbed when hydrogen-bond interactions are disrupted and released when they form. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W014 · Environmental moderation**  
Water’s high specific heat contributes to moderation of environmental temperatures and reduced temperature fluctuations in organisms. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
Energy input, hydrogen-bond rearrangement, and relatively slow temperature change must remain causally connected. The story should show the consequence at organismal or environmental scale without implying that hydrogen bonds permanently lock water molecules together.

### Scene 5 · Evaporation Balcony

**MO-APBIO-U1-W015 · High heat of vaporization**  
Water requires substantial energy to evaporate. This high heat of vaporization supports evaporative cooling and temperature maintenance. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W016 · Evaporative cooling**  
As water molecules evaporate, higher-energy molecules leave the liquid surface and carry energy away, lowering the average energy of the remaining surface. The surface cools. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W017 · Biological and environmental cooling examples**  
Evaporation of water contributes to cooling in biological and environmental contexts, including sweating and water loss from leaves. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
The student must be able to follow the energy. Evaporation requires substantial energy, higher-energy molecules escape, and the remaining liquid cools.

### Scene 6 · Ice Lake

**MO-APBIO-U1-W018 · Ice is less dense than liquid water**  
Ice is less dense than liquid water because freezing organizes water molecules into an open hydrogen-bonded lattice that spaces them farther apart than in the liquid. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W019 · Ice hydrogen-bond lattice**  
In ice, hydrogen bonds stabilize an open crystalline lattice in which each water molecule can hydrogen-bond with four neighboring water molecules. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W020 · Floating ice supports aquatic life**  
The lower density of ice allows it to float, leaving liquid water beneath that can support aquatic life. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
The open lattice must cause the lower density, the lower density must cause floating, and floating surface ice must connect to the persistence of liquid habitat below.

### Scene 7 · Solution Bar

**MO-APBIO-U1-W021 · Solvent**  
A solvent is the dissolving agent or medium. Water can dissolve many ionic and polar substances because of its polarity. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W022 · Solution**  
A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W023 · Solute**  
A solute is the substance dissolved in a solution. Exact term required.

Narrative obligation  
The three roles must remain visually and conceptually distinct. The student should know which material is the solvent, which is the solute, and what the complete homogeneous mixture is called.

### Scene 8 · Hydration Aquarium

**MO-APBIO-U1-W024 · Like dissolves like**  
A useful solubility heuristic is “like dissolves like.” Polar solvents such as water tend to interact well with ions and polar substances. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W025 · Hydration of ions**  
Around cations, water’s oxygen side is oriented inward. Around anions, water’s hydrogen sides are oriented inward. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
Water orientation around ions must follow charge. Oxygen’s partially negative side faces cations, while the partially positive hydrogen sides face anions. “Like dissolves like” must remain a heuristic rather than a universal law.

### Scene 9 · pH Staircase

**MO-APBIO-U1-W026 · pH**  
pH is a logarithmic measure related to hydrogen-ion concentration, conventionally expressed as pH = −log[H+]. Lower pH corresponds to greater acidity and higher pH to greater basicity. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W027 · Acid**  
An acid increases the hydrogen-ion concentration of a solution, commonly by donating H+. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W028 · Base**  
A base reduces hydrogen-ion concentration, for example by accepting H+ or by contributing OH− that combines with H+. Exact term required.

**MO-APBIO-U1-W029 · Water dissociation**  
Water self-ionizes to hydronium, H₃O⁺, and hydroxide, OH⁻. In aqueous biological chemistry, H+ is commonly used as shorthand for proton-related concentration. The proton should not be represented as simply floating alone in water. Exact-name retrieval is not required.

Narrative obligation  
The logarithmic pH relationship, acid/base effects on hydrogen-ion concentration, and water self-ionization must remain scientifically distinct even when they occur in one continuous crisis.

### Scene 10 · Buffer Gate

**MO-APBIO-U1-W030 · Buffer**  
A buffer resists changes in pH when acid or base is added and helps maintain pH stability in biological systems. Exact term required.

Narrative obligation  
The buffer must visibly reduce the magnitude of a pH disturbance. It must not be portrayed as preventing all pH change or as permanently neutralizing every acid or base input.

## Causal spine lock

The rewrite must preserve the scientific dependency chain even if the dramatic plot is substantially redesigned.

Water’s unequal O–H electron sharing creates polarity and partial charges. Polarity enables hydrogen-bond interactions. Hydrogen bonding contributes to cohesion and surface tension. Cohesion and adhesion together contribute to capillary action. Hydrogen-bond interactions also contribute to water’s high specific heat and high heat of vaporization. Evaporation can remove higher-energy molecules and cool a surface. Hydrogen bonding creates the open lattice of ice, making ice less dense than liquid water and allowing surface ice to float. Water’s polarity supports interaction with ions and many polar solutes, including charge-dependent orientation around cations and anions. Water chemistry then connects to H+, H₃O⁺, OH⁻, acids, bases, pH, and buffer action.

The finished story should let a student reconstruct that chain from events and images, not from an end-of-scene lecture.

## Narrative quality lock inherited from Z2 V3.2

Z2 V3.2 establishes the minimum quality floor for Z3.

- The fiction must read as one sustained adventure with escalating consequences.
- Mara must remain a character with agency, reactions, judgment, and continuity. She cannot function as a walking glossary.
- The second-person student must notice, infer, decide, predict, or act. The student cannot simply follow Mara from display to display.
- Scientific behavior must alter the physical problem, choices, danger, route, or solution.
- Locations must be spatially clear before action depends on them.
- Scene transitions must preserve geography and causal continuity.
- Terms should emerge after or during meaningful phenomena whenever possible.
- Exposition must vary in form and rhythm. Repeating apparatus → definition → verification → door-open patterns is prohibited.
- Visual memory cues must be vivid enough to retrieve the science later while remaining part of the fictional world.
- Later scenes must maintain the same prose quality, spatial clarity, characterization, and dramatic energy as the opening.
- No meta language may refer to slides, source files, curriculum records, locked content, Memory Objects, or the act of teaching the student.
- Scientific metaphors cannot create a misconception that the surrounding prose leaves unresolved.
